## 1. What chrF/BLEU do SIL's and others' drafting systems reach for a language with a complete NT, and how do they evaluate?

### Takeaway
The one systematic public benchmark is still SIL's eBible corpus paper (2023): NLLB-600M fine-tuned on one NT reaches chrF3
43-56 on held-out NT books (Romans, Revelation) and 52-60 on random held-out verses, with NLLB-1.3B adding +2 to +3 chrF3;
NT-trained models drafting Tanakh books are reported in BLEU only (6-24), which shows the NT-to-Tanakh domain penalty. The one 2026
academic result for exactly our setting (NT-only training, draft Genesis) is Dhao: NMT alone 27.1 chrF++, 35.2 after an LLM
post-edit with retrieved examples. SIL's production (Scripture Forge) reports no metrics publicly: drafts get a three-colour
usefulness rating from automated metrics, and the published benefit is throughput (about 1,000 more verses drafted and
reviewed per project per year).

### Cited Findings
**eBible corpus benchmarks (SIL, 2023; primary; figures read from the ar5iv rendering of the paper)**
- Corpus: 1,009 translations in 833 languages, 75 families; benchmarks on eight source-to-target pairings, NLLB-600M fine-tuned for all, NLLB-1.3B-distilled for three. Training sizes: Hausa-Dangaléat 7,394 verses; Kuanua-Kandas 7,404; Nepali-Tamang 7,419; Tok Pisin-Yopno 9,775 (Trans-New Guinea); Spanish-Zapotec Tabaa 9,901; Swahili-Kwere 13,304 (Bantu); Tamil-Malayalam 30,589; Hindi-E. Panjabi 30,599 — [The eBible Corpus, arXiv 2304.09919 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.09919); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.09919).
- Cross-validation (250 random test verses, 5-fold median), NLLB-600M, BLEU/spBLEU/chrF3: Dangaléat 29.9/38.4/52.5; Kandas 35.1/39.4/54.2; Malayalam 21.7/39.8/58.2; E. Panjabi 30.5/40.4/55.7; Kwere 28.8/37.9/60.4; Zapotec 28.3/44.3/56.4; Tamang 31.5/49.5/58.9; Yopno 31.6/49.0/60.1 (Table 17) — [eBible](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.09919).
- "NT completion" (train all NT except ROM and REV; test ROM | REV), NLLB-600M chrF3: Dangaléat 43.7 | 43.3; Kandas 44.9 | 50.3; Malayalam 53.1 | 54.6; E. - "Early OT" (train all NT; test GEN, EXO, LEV, NUM, DEU, RUT, PSA, JON), NLLB-600M, BLEU only: Malayalam GEN 11.8, EXO 8.9, LEV 6.4, NUM 6.5, DEU 6.4, RUT 8.1, PSA 10.9, JON 9.2; E. - "Late OT" (whole Bible minus the minor prophets) vs "Early OT" (NT only), BLEU on minor prophets, Malayalam: HOS 10.4 -> 16.6, JOL 10.2 -> 19.2, AMO 7.2 -> 17.9, HAG 6.9 -> 21.3, MAL 8.2 -> 11.9; E. Panjabi HOS 16.6 -> 22.6, HAG 17.9 -> 34.3, ZEP 19.1 -> 29.2 (Table 19) — [eBible](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.09919).
- Model size, CV BLEU/spBLEU/chrF3, 600M -> 1.3B: Malayalam 21.7/40.0/59.5 -> 24.9/44.2/62.7; Kwere 28.3/37.9/60.5 -> 29.9/40.0/62.2; Tamang 31.5/49.5/57.9 -> 33.1/51.0/59.9 (Table 15). Architectures (Table 16): SMT 36.9-49.2 chrF3, OpenNMT Transformer 37.4-46.9, NLLB-600M 57.9-60.4, NLLB-1.3B 59.9-62.7. - The paper found "no clear correlation between the scope (NT-only, NT with partial Tanakh, or full Bible) of the translation pairing and our selected scoring metrics" (snippet) — [eBible PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.09919).

**Dhao (Austronesian, ~5,000 speakers; University of Melbourne, submitted 15 Jan 2026; primary)**
- NLLB-200-distilled-600M fine-tuned on the complete NT (7,644 verses, 95/5 split), source English (WEB); test = first 500 verses of Genesis. spBLEU/chrF++: in-domain NT reference 25.19/36.17; NMT-only on Genesis 7.66/27.11; Gemini 2.5 Flash direct 0-shot 2.98/18.84, 5-shot 7.37/22.95; LLM post-edit of the NMT draft without retrieval 0-shot 10.65/27.94, 5-shot 12.54/29.62; with the full lexicon 16.27/31.32; with word-level retrieved sentences (n=10) 18.93/35.28; final (sentences + lexicon) 19.88/35.21. OOV rate rises from 8.1% (NT) to 25.9% (Genesis) — [Context Volume Drives Performance, arXiv 2601.09982](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09982v1).

**SIL model cards (David Baines, silnlp conventions; primary; undated)**

- A companion card reports German at 39.3 chrF3 on 36 books and states that "chrF3 is the headline metric", scored "at the corpus level for each held-out book, following silnlp and sacreBLEU conventions" (snippet) — [ebible_m2m-ms8-ie-shareable](https://huggingface.co/DavidCBaines/ebible_m2m-ms8-ie-shareable).

**Scripture Forge / Serval in production (SIL; primary pages)**
- Draft generation "requires a project with about 8000 verses across multiple genres"; "Paratext data from September 2024 to September 2025 indicates that projects using draft generation drafted and reviewed an average of 1,000 more verses than projects that did not". No chrF or rating figures on the page — [ai.sil.org, Scripture Forge](https://ai.sil.org/projects/scriptureforge).
- Evaluation shown to users is a three-colour rating per draft: Green "likely to be useful", Yellow "probably useful", Red "may not be useful"; the guidelines rank books: the Synoptics draft best, John lower, Acts "usually the lowest of the Gospels and Acts"; Hebrews and 2 Corinthians usually lowest among the Epistles; 1-3 John and 1-2 Thessalonians often highest. The quality figures (Figures 1-4) are images — [Guidelines for New Testament Draft Generation](https://help.scriptureforge.org/new-testament-drafting).
- The quality warning comes from automated metrics: "Sometimes these metrics indicate that draft quality may be poor"; it "may not reflect human judgment"; the commonest cause is too little suitable training text, usually a setup error (project, language codes, draft books, training books, back translations, additional data). Training books should be checked for "consistency in spelling, punctuation, and content" — [Scripture Forge FAQ](https://help.scriptureforge.org/faq).
- "All of the drafts that are created contain errors that need to be corrected by the translation team"; for some projects "the quality will be too low to be useful"; success is "their usefulness as a tool to assist the translation team in their work, not their ability to stand alone as a finished product". The model "works on individual sentences", so long multi-verse ranges "may be cut short and not drafted in full". Generation "typically takes roughly an hour and a half" — [Draft Generation (Understanding drafts)](https://help.scriptureforge.org/understanding-drafts).
- Scale, mid-2025: AI drafting "implemented or in preparation in nearly 500 translation projects worldwide" (secondary, citing wng.org) — [faith.tools](https://faith.tools/app/scripture-forge).
- Team ratings in the field (ETEN Lab posts, seen only as search snippets; the pages 404 on fetch; undated): Upper Guinea Crioulo revision test on Ruth and Jonah, "most verses were noted as acceptable-to-excellent by the team"; an Esther evaluation called the output "a viable first draft with minor issues" — [Scripture Forge Test on Upper Guinea Crioulo](https://etenlab.substack.com/p/scripture-forge-test-on-upper-guinea). SIL Tanzania workshop with 12 teams / 27 participants: teams "preferred combining drafts from model pairings with partial-book drafting"; UI language was a barrier — [Translation Technology: From Data to Drafting](https://etenlab.substack.com/p/translation-technology-from-data).

**Our own figures, for the comparison (local files)**
- Kuni (kvg) NLLB-600M fine-tuned on 7,682 NT verses, held-out Ruth: chrF3 35.4 / 35.7, BLEU 8.5 / 9.3, chrF 60.1-60.5; our agents 62.8 chrF, silnlp-style in-context drafts 64.3 (NT-only pool). Bern German NLLB on John: chrF3 54.9, BLEU 31.1, below our agents in 20 of 21 chapters by 2.5 chrF / 5.0 chrF3; NLLB-vs-agent disagreement as triage: AUC 0.73 for the agent's weakest quarter — `internal-docs/sil-drafting-study/nllb.md`.

### Inferences
- The band our Papuan NLLB sits in (chrF3 ~35 on a Tanakh book from an NT-only model) matches Dhao's NMT-only Genesis (27.1 chrF++; chrF++ runs a few points under chrF3) and eBible's NT-to-Tanakh BLEU collapse (Malayalam CV BLEU 21.7 -> Genesis 11.8, Leviticus 6.4). eBible's 43-60 chrF3 figures are in-domain NT tests and are not the number to expect for Tanakh drafting from an NT.
- The Ndebele card's 53-56 chrF3 on Ruth/Jonah/Genesis 1 most likely reflects multi-source Bantu training with NLLB-native sources (Shona, Swahili); it is the nearest published figure for a Bantu target like Zinza, and suggests that a Swahili or Luganda source column could lift an NLLB second-draft model for Zinza well above the Kuni level. (speculation)
- SIL's public yardsticks are throughput and a three-colour usefulness rating; their per-verse confidence (a projected chrF3 with a two-Gaussian usable/unusable split, known from the code) is not described on any public page found. A "percentage of verses kept without edit" metric would be new in the public record.

### Gaps
- No SIL report tabulating chrF3 across N languages for 2024-2026; no HTER, post-edit time or "verses kept" figures for Scripture Forge drafts anywhere public.
- eBible's chrF3 for the NT-only-to-Tanakh task is not in the paper's tables (BLEU only), and its three smallest pairings (~7.4k verses, the Kuni-sized ones) have no Tanakh rows.
- The silnlp wiki (NMT: Usage, Configure a model) and papers or talks by Daspit, Martin, Shannon or Woodward were not reached; the README itself gives no metrics — [silnlp README](https://github.com/sillsdev/silnlp).
- "Omnilingual MT: Machine Translation for 1,600 Languages" (arXiv 2603.16309, 2026) surfaced but was not read; it may carry Bible-domain figures.

## 2. What did the 2024-2026 reports find helps most?

### Takeaway
Measured gains, in order: more and broader target text (eBible: +5 to +14 BLEU on the minor prophets from NT-only to whole-Bible training; NT-completion beats gospel-only by 10+ BLEU); a larger base model (+2-3 chrF3); and, for the NT-to-Tanakh shift, LLM post-editing of an NMT draft with many retrieved examples (Dhao: +8.1 chrF++, almost back to the in-domain score), where the volume of examples matters more than the retrieval method. Related-language transfer is mixed; grammar explanations add little next to parallel examples. Key-term enforcement, back-translation and QE routing appear as workflow features, not as measured gains.

### Cited Findings
- Training breadth: "increased size and literary breadth of the training set is beneficial"; stage-by-stage BLEU on ROM/REV, Dangaléat: gospel-trained 7.0/7.6 -> epistle-trained 14.1/15.1 -> NT-completion 18.9/16.0; Yopno 8.8/7.4 -> 18.5/18.3 -> 24.9/22.1 (Table 3) — [eBible](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.09919).
- Related language: "results were mixed"; Austronesian (Kandas + Ramoaaina) gained up to +10.2 BLEU on epistles (1TH 20.1 -> 30.0) and REV chrF3 50.3 -> 58.0; Afro-Asiatic, Indo-European, Sino-Tibetan and Trans-New Guinea pairings were flat or negative (Tamang REV chrF3 53.2 -> 54.0 but gospel task 56.1 -> 53.9); gains track HMM alignment scores between target and related language (Austronesian 0.68, the highest) — [eBible](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.09919).
- Model size: NLLB-1.3B-distilled "consistently better for each translation pairing than with the NLLB-600M model" (+2.0 to +3.2 chrF3) — [eBible](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.09919).
- Retrieval + LLM post-edit (Dhao, 2026): "performance is driven primarily by the number of retrieved examples rather than the choice of retrieval algorithm"; sentence-level retrieval (BGE, BM25, ChrF-RAG) plateaus at ~60 examples, word-level retrieval keeps improving to an effective ~137 examples; full-lexicon retrieval improves monotonically; the LLM alone "cannot translate Dhao" (2.98 spBLEU 0-shot) but post-editing the NMT draft "fixes repetition loops, named-entity hallucinations, and some idioms" — [Context Volume Drives Performance](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09982v1).
- Grammar data: adding the grammar book's sentences to fine-tuning slightly hurt (chrF++ 27.11 -> 26.62); retrieving only from the NT (no grammar book) costs 0.27 chrF++ (35.28 -> 35.01); recommendation: use grammar material as a retrieval resource in the prompt, not as training data — [Context Volume Drives Performance](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09982v1).
- Grammar books vs examples (Aycock et al., arXiv 2409.19151, revised April 2025): "almost all of the improvement comes from the parallel examples rather than the grammatical explanations"; a fine-tuned encoder-decoder can match the grammar-book LLM result (snippet) — [Can LLMs really learn to translate from one grammar book?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19151v1).
- Many-shot in-context MT for ten low-resource languages (2026): chrF++ scaling curves with the number of examples; BM25 to choose fewer, better examples; cost is the main obstacle; Bible use not visible in the snippet — [Many-Shot ICL for low-resource MT, arXiv 2604.02596](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02596v2).
- SIL's own levers in practice: drafts "generated from previously translated Scripture" to "maintain consistency and naturalness"; planned "research-based draft generation processes" for projects under the data minimum — [ai.sil.org](https://ai.sil.org/projects/scriptureforge). Incremental drafting (retrain after 8-10 translated chapters of the current book) gives "significant improvement" for the Gospels and Acts (use it once for Mark and John, twice for Matthew, Luke and Acts); Acts should be drafted before the Epistles; epistles one at a time; numbered sets in order except 2 Timothy before 1 Timothy; a draft-optimised epistle order (1JN, 2JN, 3JN, 1TH, 2TH, REV, 1CO, 2TI, GAL, 1PE, PHP, ROM, TIT, EPH, PHM, JAS, 2PE, JUD, 1TI, COL, HEB, 2CO); start with Matthew or Luke rather than Mark — [NT drafting guidelines](https://help.scriptureforge.org/new-testament-drafting). "The more parallel text you have, the better"; the parallel sentences "need to say the same thing in both languages"; a back translation, "usually much more literal", is easier for the system, and back-translation drafts are "substantially higher" in quality than vernacular drafts; after editing one book, "the system would use the translation of Genesis to improve the quality of the next draft" — [Understanding drafts](https://help.scriptureforge.org/understanding-drafts).
- Practitioners (SIL Tanzania workshop): "combining drafts from model pairings with partial-book drafting" (snippet) — [ETEN Lab](https://etenlab.substack.com/p/translation-technology-from-data).

### Inferences
- The Dhao design (NMT draft + LLM revision with a lexicon and many retrieved verses) is the same shape as our in-context second drafts and verse packs; its +8 chrF++ is larger than the +2-4 chrF we measured, but its LLM had the NMT draft and a lexicon in the prompt, which ours does not. The "volume over method" result argues for larger packs before cleverer retrieval.
- eBible's related-language result predicts little from a Trans-New Guinea neighbour for Kuni (the only Anim text is Kuni's own) and possibly something for Zinza from a well-aligned Rutara or Swahili source, which is what the Ndebele card shows.
- SIL's "incremental drafting" is our "chapter after chapter, each checked in the loop", measured by them as a significant gain on narrative books; it is the one human-in-the-loop effect anyone reports.

### Gaps
- No public ablation from SIL or silnlp (2024-2026) on multi-source models, key-term constraints or quality-estimation routing.
- No measurement anywhere of "draft, revise, redraft" loops on draft quality beyond SIL's qualitative "significant improvement".

## 3. Other organisations' practice and the human side of evaluation

### Takeaway
Public evaluation practice is qualitative and tool-level: the Greek Room (USC ISI) checks characters, spelling and alignment-based consistency; consultant guidance lists the error classes to check in AI drafts (names, numbers, kinship terms, participant roles, pronouns, connectors, figurative language) and warns that AI drafts "look natural and consistent" and invite complacency; vendors report self-estimated time savings (75-80%); no peer-reviewed evaluation of Greek Room, Avodah or AQuA exists, and unfoldingWord's AI stance did not surface.

### Cited Findings
- Greek Room (Ulf Hermjakob and Joel Mathew, USC ISI): Wildebeest checks "a wide range of character level problems"; spell-checking "based on phonetic string similarity and word alignment" rather than word lists; a word-alignment view whose colour-coded strengths flag "unusual translations, missing or spurious words"; output is advisory; runs after sign-up at app.greekroom.org; licence not stated — [Greek Room](https://greekroom.org/); news coverage: aimed at "very low-resource languages that are not even in the top 500" — [ChurchLeaders/RNS, 2023](https://churchleaders.com/news/454365-usc-researchers-use-ai-to-help-translate-bible-into-very-rare-languages-rns.html).
- Consultant guidance (Google Doc, attributed by search to Seed Company; read only in snippets): AI drafts commonly contain "incorrect names, numbers, kinship terms, word choices, participant roles, and/or pronouns", grammar and "logical-connector" errors and misread figurative language; "these common errors also occur in human drafts"; AI drafts "look natural and consistent, which has the potential to encourage less diligence in checking"; "the normal QA steps still apply" — [Guidance doc (PDF export)](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1HSwkrGcfUPet354CLzV_dthi_phGioNR/export?format=pdf).
- Consultants' needs (user study, 2023): they "struggled with consistency across books" and wanted tools that flag inconsistent term usage "based on context and inflections, just in time" — [User Study for Improving Tools for Bible Translation, arXiv 2302.00778](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.00778).
- Avodah / KIN International / Seed Company / Biblica (Nov 2025, self-reported): translation time "reduced by 75 to 80 percent", costs by up to 60%; models "trained on portions of Scripture already manually translated by humans to provide a first draft", humans "still be involved, especially for quality checks" — [Religion Unplugged](https://religionunplugged.com/news/2025/11/12/how-artificial-intelligence-is-transforming-bible-translation).
- SIL described as developing "AI tools for quality assessment" that flag "common translation challenges" for consultants; no method given — [The Gospel Coalition](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/ai-bible-translation/). AQuA: only a Houghton University note on an SIL data-science project "to make efficient tools for Biblical translation" — [Houghton](https://www.houghton.edu/?p=23932).
- Similarity scoring between translations as a technique (Targum corpus, 2026): cosine similarity of Qwen3-Embedding-8B chapter embeddings plus Levenshtein, because embeddings "can miss minor textual variations" — [Targum, arXiv 2602.09724](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.09724).
- Cherokee NT verses with GPT-4o, GPT-4o+RAG and Llama 3.1 405B: verse-level, outputs diverge heavily from the reference (snippet) — [Transcending Language Boundaries, arXiv 2411.11295](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.11295).
- ETEN Innovation Lab: "discovery, testing, scaling of emerging Bible translation methods"; monthly digest — [ETEN Lab](https://etenlab.substack.com/), [Digest June 2025](https://etenlab.substack.com/p/bible-translation-innovation-digest); SIL partnered with XRI Global and a Tanzanian team as "research partners"; lack of reliable data named the main constraint (snippet) — [Wycliffe Global Alliance](https://wycliffe.net/?p=886).
- unfoldingWord: only a 2024 MNN piece quoting Birch Champeon as having "considered some of these exact issues" — [MNN, Aug 2024](https://www.mnnonline.org/news/could-ai-help-with-bible-translation/).
- Quality criteria in SIL practice: accuracy, clarity, naturalness, with acceptability proposed as a fourth (oral translation article, snippet) — [Acta Theologica](https://journals.ufs.ac.za/index.php/at/article/view/10839).

### Inferences
- The error classes named by consultants (names, numbers, kinship, participant roles, pronouns, connectors) are a ready checklist; our loop already reviews negations, names and copied text, and nobody publicly reports a negation or quote-consistency check, so those are ours to describe.
- The 75-80% time-saving figure is an executive's estimate for a vendor pipeline, not a measured post-edit study.

### Gaps
- AQuA's method and validation; unfoldingWord's "Open Components" and checking tools; Wycliffe Associates, Kalaam/Prophet, Tiny Translator, Biblica's quality assessment: nothing found in budget.
- No published Likert distributions, pairwise preferences, comprehension-test results or post-edit times for AI Bible drafts.

## 4. Discourse-level work: particles, connectives, participant reference in MT/AI-assisted translation; SIL discourse analyses for Papuan and Bantu languages

### Takeaway
No published MT or LLM Bible-translation work checks discourse features: the published checks are character-, spelling-, alignment- and verse-level. The usable instruments are the human discourse analyses: Levinsohn's method (print/Logos), Nicolle's eastern Bantu syntheses (one open PDF), the SIL Uganda-Tanzania Branch "X Narrative Discourse" write-ups for Mara-cluster and other Tanzanian languages, and for Papuan languages de Vries on tail-head linkage and Roberts on switch-reference. Nothing exists on Zinza, Boazi, Kuni or Zimakani discourse; for Kuni the model has to be Marind (Olsson) plus the areal literature, tested on the NT.

### Cited Findings
**MT / AI (2022-2026)**
- Greek Room: nothing on discourse, connectives, pronouns or document-level consistency (page read) — [Greek Room](https://greekroom.org/).
- Dhao 2026: verse-level metrics only; the LLM is a "backstop" for severe NMT failures; no discourse feature evaluated — [arXiv 2601.09982](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09982v1). eBible 2023: verse-level benchmarks, no discourse measure — [arXiv 2304.09919](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.09919).
- General-domain document-level LLM MT: Wang et al. 2023 (ChatGPT/GPT-4 ahead of commercial MT in human evaluation on whole documents) — [arXiv 2304.02210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02210v2); a six-LLM, seven-pair literary study finds "translate the whole document, then refine sentence by sentence" the most stable gain and argues BLEU misleads at document level (title not confirmed; seen via the author page) — [ACL Anthology, Dawei Zhu](https://aclanthology.org/people/dawei-zhu-8854/).
- Older background: "Document Authoring the Bible for Minority Language Translation", MT Summit 2005 — [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2005.mtsummit-papers.9).

**Method and Bantu applications**
- Levinsohn, S. ### Inferences
- Reading order for the Zinza brief's verb section: Muzale 1998's Zinza chapter (open; OCR needed), then Hewson/Nurse/Muzale 2000 for Haya staged T/A (open), then Rugemalira 2005 if the UDSM record carries a PDF; Odden 1998 is the only verb-tone-by-tense analysis in a sister language and must be asked for. For discourse: Nicolle 2014 (open) and Nicolle 2015 (SIL EWP, direct PDF URL to be found), then the Luganda ne- narrative tense and Rukiga historical present as competing hypotheses.
- Public-domain or open relatives usable as extra corpus columns: the Kerewe 1946 NT (public domain; JE24, a Rutara sister of Zinza in Glottolog's grouping), Kamoga & Stevick (public domain, tone-marked Luganda), BLR3 roots. The Ndebele card (section 1) suggests a well-aligned Bantu source column lifts an NLLB model; the Kerewe NT is the natural test.
- The NT's tone marking (acute on H, rising and falling; L unmarked) follows the 2015 orthography statement, so the corpus carries surface tone: rare and useful for separating tense forms that differ by tone alone (Odom's naahika / naáhika).
