# Source datasets for a universal per-clause specification of the Hebrew Bible and Greek NT

Researched 2026-10-22. Local inventories were taken from the files `scripts/setup.py` pins in this repo
(`download/macula-hebrew/47db250bd55d/*-lowfat.xml`, `download/macula-greek/8423afe47b9e/*.xml`,
`orig/heb.sqlite`, `orig/grc.sqlite`, `download/hebrew-speaker-layer/hebrew-speaker-layer-v0.11/README.md`);
"local inventory" below means a count over those files, run today. Web facts carry the date of the page where it
gives one; older facts are marked.

## KQ1. What each dataset encodes at clause and word level, in which format, under which licence

### Takeaway
Only MACULA Hebrew/Greek (CC BY 4.0), Clear's speaker-quotations (CC BY 4.0), bcv-commons' two layers (CC BY-SA / CC BY),
OpenText.org's GNT_annotation_v1.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0) and STEPBible (CC BY 4.0) are publishable; the BHSA, PROIEL and the UD
Ancient Hebrew treebank are non-commercial; the Westminster Hebrew Syntax is CC BY-NC-ND at the Groves Center but CC BY 4.0
as re-released inside MACULA; the Lexham discourse datasets are proprietary (Logos). No open dataset gives a clause-to-matrix
relation in semantic terms for the NT; for the Tanakh only hebrew-clause-layout does (rule-derived), and BHSA (non-commercial)
gives distributional codes.

- Clear's Greek documentation: "Eight categories of clause level function are distinguished, using the following labels: ADV, IO, O, O2, P, S, V, VC"; ClType: default verbal; "Verbless Clause" = "relational clauses of identification or attribution without an explicit copula verb"; "Verb Elided Clause" = clauses that "imply the carrying over of Verbal Function"; "Minor Clause" = clauses "without any predication" (vocatives, interjections, left-dislocated NPs); `Conj-CL` for coordinators above sentence level and sentence adverbials "left connected only to the sentences that follow them"; `sub-CL` joins a subordinating conjunction to its clause; `ClCl`/`ClCl2` connect a subordinate or vocative clause to a main clause (first / second clause is head); `PtclCL` attaches ἄν to the whole clause; `NP-CL` attaches a relative clause to its antecedent; "Direct speech is treated the same way as indirect speech" (own clause, ClCl or separate sentence); genitive absolutes "connect as separate clauses to their main clauses", typically ClCl2, the genitive noun marked as Subject of the participle; a participle that "further specifies the action of a verb" is ADV "unless it further restricts the reference of the noun" (then an adjp inside the NP); argument frames are "a later stage of work" — [MACULA Greek Treebank doc (PDF, read via a text proxy)](https://github.com/Clear-Bible/macula-greek/blob/main/doc/MACULA%20Greek%20Treebank%20for%20the%20Nestle%201904%20Greek%20New%20Testament.pdf)

## KQ2. Inter-clause relation vocabularies and a mapping to a universal set

### Takeaway
Three vocabularies exist: BHSA's distributional `code` groups + functional `rela`, MACULA's constituent `rule` names (which encode attachment, not meaning), and hebrew-clause-layout's 17 semantic `relation` values (Tanakh only). PROIEL/UD give a fourth, syntactic one (ADV/COMP/ATR/APOS; advcl/ccomp/acl/parataxis). ISO 24617-8 (DR-core) and UD are the natural pivot sets; none of the open datasets labels concession, result or manner for the NT.

### Cited Findings
- BHSA `code` groups: relative, infinitive construct (by preposition), asyndetic, parallel, defective, conjunctive adverbs, coordinate, postulational, conditional, temporal, final, causal, direct speech; declared "of a distributional, not functional, nature" — [code](https://etcbc.github.io/bhsa/features/code/); BHSA `rela`: Adju, Attr, Cmpl, Coor, Objc, PrAd, PreC, ReVo, Resu, RgRc, Spec, Subj — [rela](https://etcbc.github.io/bhsa/features/rela/)
- MACULA Hebrew clause `rule` values are constituent patterns (`V-S-O`, `V-PP`, `S-P`) or attachment rules (`CLaCL`, `ClCl`, `ClCl2`, `cjpCLx`, `relCL`, `Relp2CL`, `O2CL`, `S2CL`, `ADV2CL`, `PtclCL`, `Intj2CL`, `Conj3CL`, `CLandCL2`); the only role a clause bears toward its matrix is `wg@role` (s, o, o2, p, adv, pp) — local inventory; the Greek set likewise (`Conj-CL`, `sub-CL`, `CLaCL`, `DetCL`, `that-VP`, `PtclCL`, `aCLaCL`) with clause roles s/o/io/adv/p — local inventory; Clear's doc: ClCl/ClCl2 "connect a subordinate or vocative clause to a main clause" — [MACULA Greek doc](https://github.com/Clear-Bible/macula-greek/blob/main/doc/MACULA%20Greek%20Treebank%20for%20the%20Nestle%201904%20Greek%20New%20Testament.pdf)
- hebrew-clause-layout `relation`: continuation, coordination, asyndetic, speech, inquit, causal, purpose, temporal, conditional, relative, circumstantial, complement, vocative, embedded, parallel (17 values in the released schema); `discourse_rule` 27 values; the rules live in `clause_layout.toml` — [card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/bcv-commons/hebrew-clause-layout), [local card copy](/home/lgunnars/dev/bsb-add-books-work/scratch/template/bsb-add-books/docs/proposals/hebrew-clause-layout/README.md)
- PROIEL: subordinate clauses hang on their subjunction, which is COMP (verb-selected), ADV (adjunct) or APOS (on a noun); conjunct participles XADV; infinitives COMP or XOBJ/XADV — [annotation guide](http://dev.syntacticus.org/annotation-guide/); UD grc_proiel relations acl, advcl, advcl:cmp, ccomp, csubj, xcomp, parataxis, conj, mark — [UD grc_proiel](https://universaldependencies.org/treebanks/grc_proiel/index.html); UD hbo_ptnk uses advcl, acl:relcl, compound:smixut — [UD hbo_ptnk](https://universaldependencies.org/treebanks/hbo_ptnk/index.html)
- ISO 24617-8:2016 (DR-core) "establishes the representation and annotation of local, 'low-level' discourse relations between situations", "a set of core discourse relations, many of which have similar definitions in different frameworks", last reviewed and confirmed 2022 — [iso.org/standard/60780](https://www.iso.org/standard/60780.html); a 2017 Bunt–Prasad deck lists Cause, Condition, Negative Condition, Concession, Purpose among the core relations, each with two arguments (Cause: Reason/Result) — [search summary of LTRF-16.01.2017](https://LT.cityu.edu.hk/Research/cred/repo/2017/LTRF-16.01.2017.pdf); a 2023 comparison places ISO Cause/Condition/Negative Condition/Purpose under Contingency and Concession under Comparison — [arXiv 2302.12944](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.12944); Prasad & Bunt 2015 identify 20 core relations — [ACL W15-0210](https://aclanthology.org/W15-0210/)

### Inferences
- A workable mapping (my proposal from the value lists above): universal `condition` ← BHSA code 600-667 / layout `conditional` / PROIEL ADV+εἰ,ἐάν / UD advcl+mark; `purpose` ← code 800-867 ("final"), layout `purpose`, PROIEL XADV infinitive or ADV+ἵνα, UD advcl; `result` ← no BHSA/layout value (ὥστε clauses sit under code 300-367 or ADV); `cause` ← code 900-967, layout `causal`, ADV+ὅτι/γάρ; `time` ← code 700-767, layout `temporal`, ADV/XADV (genitive absolute); `relative` ← code 10-16, rela Attr, layout `relative`, PROIEL ATR/APOS, UD acl:relcl; `complement` ← rela Objc/Subj/Cmpl, layout `complement`, PROIEL COMP, UD ccomp/csubj; `quotation` ← code 999, layout `speech`/`inquit`, speaker layers; `coordination` ← code 400-487 / 200-201, layout `coordination`/`parallel`/`continuation`, UD conj/parataxis; `concession` and `manner` have no source value anywhere and need a lexical rule (אַף כִּי, גַּם, καίπερ, participle of manner) or a model judgment.
- MACULA's `rule` strings carry the clause's syntactic role toward its matrix (a `wg class="cl" role="adv"` is an adverbial clause) but not its meaning; the conjunction lemma (`כִּי`, `אִם`, `לְמַעַן`, ὅτι, ἵνα, ὥστε, ἐάν) plus mood is what a script would read.

### Gaps
- The full ISO DR-core relation list with argument roles was not obtained from a primary source (PDFs returned binary; the deck URL now 404s).
- The 17 `relation` values of hebrew-clause-layout's release were not all visible in the viewer (the card lists 14 proposed + `parallel`; the rest come from later versions and should be read from the pinned `build.json`).

## KQ3. How reported speech is delimited and nested

### Takeaway
MACULA trees do not attach direct speech to its inquit; delimitation comes from Clear's speaker-quotations (per-word depth projected from eight English translations' quotation marks, FCBH characters) and, for the Tanakh, from hebrew-speaker-layer (per clause segment, with speaker, nesting level and a chapter "frame"); BHSA marks depth in `txt` and the inquit link with `code` 999.

### Cited Findings
- Clear speaker-quotations: per-word `depth` (1 narrative, 2 direct speech, ≥3 nested; 0 unaligned); nesting "differs across translations because of editorial practices"; quote types seen in the served data: Normal, Dialogue, Implicit, Quotation, Hypothetical; speaker basis block / referent / character — [README](https://github.com/Clear-Bible/speaker-quotations), local `orig/heb.sqlite`
- MACULA Hebrew doc: direct speech clauses "are not connected with the clause that introduced the speech"; MACULA Greek doc: "Direct speech is treated the same way as indirect speech" — the two Clear docs linked in KQ1
- hebrew-speaker-layer: `level` (0 none, 2+ nested), `speaker`, `inquit` (the line opening a nested quotation), `frame` ("the speaker of text that is speech from its first line (a psalmist, a prophet, a first-person narrator)"); 3,892 outer / 1,278 nested quotations; `quote_type`/`basis` values block, referent, character, layout, translations, inner inquit, oracle, messenger formula, relayed message, commanded message, song, voice, titled psalm, correction, review — [card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/bcv-commons/hebrew-speaker-layer), local README
- BHSA: `txt` sequence of N/Q/D letters reflects embedding; `code` 999 marks the speech clause atom's relation to the introducing clause atom "when a verbum dicendi introduces the speech" — [txt](https://etcbc.github.io/bhsa/features/txt/), [code](https://etcbc.github.io/bhsa/features/code/)
- hebrew-clause-layout: relations `speech` and `inquit`; `discourse_parent` chains carry speeches across verses — [local card copy](/home/lgunnars/dev/bsb-add-books-work/scratch/template/bsb-add-books/docs/proposals/hebrew-clause-layout/README.md)
- PROIEL: no quotation relation; direct speech is a clause under COMP/PRED like indirect speech (the guide names COMP for AcI and selected subjunctions) — [annotation guide](http://dev.syntacticus.org/annotation-guide/)
- Logos sells a separate "Reported Speech Dataset" (~8,000 speeches), commercial — [logos.com product 27288](https://www.logos.com/product/27288/reported-speech-dataset)

### Inferences
- For the NT the only open per-word quotation depth is speaker-quotations' projection (plus OpenText.org's NT projections inside it); a Greek speaker layer analogous to hebrew-speaker-layer does not exist yet and would be built the same way (Clear depth + inquit verbs + MACULA `subjref` of the inquit).
- Addressee is in no dataset; it can be derived from the vocative (BHSA `Voct`, Greek `case="vocative"`) and from `io` of the inquit verb (MACULA Greek `role="io"`).

### Gaps
- Clear's NT quotation coverage figures and the handling of Tanakh quotations inside the NT (Levinsohn marks Tanakh quotations; MACULA does not) were not checked.

## KQ6. What is missing from all open datasets, and known attempts to add it

### Takeaway
Missing everywhere (open or not): speech act, polarity scope, aspect/time reference of Hebrew verbs in context, result/concession/manner relations, story-line vs background as a label, addressee. Partly present: implicit participants (MACULA `subjref`), nesting of speech (speaker layers), text type (BHSA, NC). Attempts are scattered: ETCBC dissertations (Kalkman 2015 on verb forms in poetry, Naaijer 2020 on clause-structure variation, Erwich 2021 on participant coreference in Psalms, Dyk's valence project), Levinsohn's and Runge's discourse markups (the latter proprietary), and outside biblical studies ISO DR-core and UMR as target vocabularies.

### Cited Findings
- Speech act: no field in MACULA (local inventory), BHSA (feature list), PROIEL (relations), hebrew-clause-layout (fields) or speaker layers; the only form-level cues are MACULA `w@type` imperative/jussive/cohortative/interrogative/exhortation/affirmation and BHSA phrase `function` Ques — sources as in KQ1
- Aspect/time reference in context: BHSA's `vt` page says "the meanings/functions of these tenses should be clarified" — [vt](https://etcbc.github.io/bhsa/features/vt/); ETCBC's method registers surface features first, functional labels second — [search summary, JSEM article](https://unisapressjournals.co.za/index.php/JSEM/article/view/2974); Kalkman, "Verbal Forms in Biblical Hebrew Poetry: Poetic Freedom or Linguistic System?", VU Amsterdam PhD, 2015-09-21, supervisors Talstra and van Peursen — [VU record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/verbal-forms-in-biblical-hebrew-poetry-poetic-freedom-or-linguist/); Bergström, *Aspect, Communicative Appeal, and Temporal Meaning in Biblical Hebrew Verbal Forms* (Eisenbrauns 2022), corpus-based, argues for basic aspectual meanings with derived temporal readings — [search summary](https://muse.jhu.edu/book/99848); Cook, *Time and the Biblical Hebrew Verb* (Eisenbrauns 2012) — [record](https://www.relbib.de/Record/1620257238?lng=fr)
- Clause-structure variation: Naaijer, *Clause Structure Variation in Biblical Hebrew: A Quantitative Approach*, VU PhD 2020 (machine learning, statistics; co-supervisor Roorda) — [VU record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/6ff60e2a-6af9-4717-aac8-aca2f14c47cc)
- Verbal valence: ETCBC `valence` repository, MIT, "a set of higher level features in text-fabric format, storing the flowchart input data and outcomes", Janet Dyk (flowcharts) and Dirk Roorda, part of SYNVAR, 2017-09-14 — [github.com/ETCBC/valence](https://github.com/ETCBC/valence)
- Participants: Erwich, *Who is Who in the Psalms? Coreference Resolution as Exegetical Tool for Participant Analysis in Biblical Texts*, VU 2021; a Participant Tracking view exists on the Scriptura/Paratext site; no public download of the data found — [Scriptura page](https://paratext.scriptura.org/w/Who_is_Who_in_the_Psalms%3F), [Text-Fabric paper](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/text-fabric-what-how-and-why/)
- Time phrases: Kingham, "Parts of Speech in Biblical Hebrew Time Phrases: A Cognitive-Statistical Analysis" (2021), ~3,400 adverbial phrases, PCA — [Open Book Publishers](https://www.openbookpublishers.com/product/1392)
- Greek discourse: Levinsohn's complete markup (SIL 2016) and OpenGNT's mapping to it, including "clause markers, Old Testament quotations, and reported speech" — [OpenGNT fileDescription (search summary)](https://content.bibletranslationtools.org/WycliffeAssociates/OpenGNT/raw/branch/master/fileDescription.md); Runge/Westbury's Lexham devices ("20 devices that are common to all languages"), Logos only — [logos.com 81254](https://www.logos.com/product/81254/lexham-discourse-bible)
- Semantic-structure tradition: Beekman, Callow & Kopesec, *The Semantic Structure of Written Communication*, SIL 1981, "semantic relations and roles of the communication units", basis of SIL's Semantic Structure Analyses of NT books — [AbeBooks listing](https://www.abebooks.com/9781556715303/Semantic-Structure-Written-Communication-Beekman-1556715307/plp); the Semantic Structure Analyses series is "a special kind of commentary to help translators find the precise meaning of the Source Language text" — same search
- Universal target vocabularies: ISO 24617-8 DR-core (20 core relations, 2 arguments each) — [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/60780.html), [Prasad & Bunt 2015](https://aclanthology.org/W15-0210/); UMR: sentence graph + document graph with coreference, temporal and modal relations, events linked to "conceivers", aspect as a lattice meant "to generalize across typologically diverse languages", first release in six languages — [NSF PAR 10348147](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10348147), [arXiv 2603.24797 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24797); AutoAspect rule-based tense/aspect labelling 62.57% accuracy on identified events — [search summary](https://aclanthology.org/people/meagan-vigus/)
- Universal Dependencies supplies Polarity, Aspect, Mood, VerbForm, Definite features and advcl/ccomp/acl/parataxis/vocative/dislocated/discourse relations, already applied to the Greek NT (PROIEL conversion) and to a BHSA/MACULA-based Hebrew treebank — UD pages in KQ1

### Inferences
- A per-clause universal layer would have to add, by script plus rules: (a) speech act from mood + particles + question markers + vocative; (b) polarity and its scope from the negator's position in the MACULA tree (negator inside the clause `wg` vs inside an NP); (c) relation from conjunction lemma + mood + hebrew-clause-layout `relation` (Tanakh) or MACULA `role`+`sub-CL` (NT), mapped to DR-core/UD names; (d) discourse status from the speaker layers + BHSA-style text type reproduced on MACULA (narrative wayyiqtol chains vs yiqtol/participle background); (e) participants from `subjref`/`participantref`/`referent` with speaker ids from speaker-quotations; (f) TAM of Hebrew verbs as a function label chosen by rule tables per form and context, which no dataset has and which is the main model-dependent step.
- The parts the ETCBC has studied (valence, participant coreference, verb forms in poetry, clause-structure variation) are all non-commercial or unpublished as data; only the method can be reused, not the rows.

### Gaps
- No project named "Biblical Hebrew Time Reference" was found; nothing resembling a time-reference-annotated corpus of Hebrew verb forms turned up in four searches. Bergström 2022 and Kalkman 2015 are the closest corpus-based works, and their annotations are not published as data as far as the searches show.
- The Beekman–Callow relation list (addition/support, orientation, clarification, logical relations such as reason–result, means–purpose, condition–consequence, concession–contraexpectation, grounds–conclusion) could not be sourced online; it is remembered from the 1981 book and must be verified before use.
- The Levinsohn LGNTDF feature inventory and licence terms were not retrieved (GitHub page shows the folder and a licence link only).
- Whether Clear plans an open discourse or speech-act layer is unknown; the MACULA READMEs say only that "more datasets are being added one at a time".
