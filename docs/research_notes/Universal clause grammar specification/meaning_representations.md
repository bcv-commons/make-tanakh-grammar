# Meaning-level (semantic and discourse) representations of clauses for a universal per-clause Bible-translation specification

Researched 2026-10-22. Scope: UMR, AMR, UCCA, PropBank/VerbNet/FrameNet, DRS/Parallel Meaning Bank, the SIL semantic-structure tradition (Beekman–Callow–Kopesec, Larson, the SSA series), TBTA's text-meaning representations, Louw–Nida / SDBH / MARBLE, Clear Bible's MACULA layers, and discourse-feature annotation of the Greek NT and Hebrew Bible (Levinsohn, Runge, Dooley & Levinsohn, OpenText.org, BHSA text types). Local data (MACULA lowfat XML, the bcv-commons speaker layer and clause layout) was inspected directly in `/home/lgunnars/dev/bsb-add-books-work/download/`; those observations are marked "local inspection". Several primary sites were unreachable on the day (amr.isi.edu: DNS failure; pmb.let.rug.nl: connection refused; lindat UMR licence page: socket hang-up; the UTA dissertation repository: certificate error); where that left a fact unverified it is listed under Gaps.

## Key question 0 — What does each representation record at clause level, in what notation, under what licence, with what tooling and Bible coverage, and how much is derivable from syntax trees?

### Takeaway
Of all the schemes, only UMR records the full set the drafting toolkit needs (roles, aspect, modal strength, polarity, mode, person/number lattices with clusivity, intra-sentence discourse relations, and document-level temporal/modal/coreference links), in a compact PENMAN notation under public guidelines; but it has no Bible data, its 1.0 release is small (2,022 sentences) and its licence is per-graphbank (some parts non-commercial). The SIL tradition (Larson's 28 inter-propositional relations, Beekman–Callow's semantic units, TBTA's controlled-English TMRs) is the only one built for Bible translation into typologically diverse languages, but its data is either in print (SSA series, no open licence) or private (TBTA templates). MACULA (CC BY 4.0) already gives, per clause, the predicate, A0/A1 frames, participant referents, morphological TAM, negation particles, subordinators, semantic-domain senses and speaker spans; what it lacks is exactly what UMR and SSA add: clause relations above the verse, aspect/modality interpretation, speech acts, implicit participants and prominence.

### Cited Findings

**UMR (Uniform Meaning Representation)**
- UMR is a collaboration of CU Boulder, Brandeis and UNM that "extends AMR to other languages, especially morphologically complex, low-resource ones", adds features for semantic interpretation and "a document-level companion representation for coreference, temporal, and modal relations"; it is meant to be "scalable, learnable, and cross-linguistically plausible" — [UMR project site](https://umr4nlp.github.io/web/)
- The first data release (LREC-COLING 2024, 23 authors) covers six languages — Arapaho, Chinese, English, Kukama, Navajo, Sanapaná — with a sentence-level graph (predicate-argument structure, named entities, word senses, event aspect, person/number of entities) and a document-level graph (coreference, temporal and modal relations across sentences) — [Bonn et al. 2024, ACL Anthology 2024.lrec-main.229](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.229)
- UMR v1.0 totals 2,022 sentence-level UMRs across the six languages; English data are "fragmented transcripts of a spoken description of a silent film" plus LORELEI news; Chinese is Wikinews; Arapaho, Navajo and Kukama are narrative texts. UMR v2.0 (in preparation per a 2025/26 parser paper) adds Czech and Latin, 210,237 sentence-level UMRs (29,912 English, 175,268 Czech), much of the new English being Minecraft dialogue — [SETUP parser paper, arXiv 2512.07068](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07068v1)
- A 2023 project poster lists per-language counts (sentence-level / document-level): Arapaho 406/109, Chinese 358/358, English 209/202, Kukama 105/86, Navajo 522/168, Sanapaná 602/602, and says UMR 1.0 is released via LINDAT — [NSF CIRC poster (Bonn)](https://nsf-circ23.utah.edu/_resources/documents/posters/circ23-pi-mtg-poster-bonn.pdf)
- The LINDAT record (handle `http://hdl.handle.net/11234/1-5198`, publisher "UMR Consortium", publication year 2023, one tar file of about 3 MB) is governed by the "Uniform Meaning Representation v1.0 License Agreement" — [b2find record](https://b2find.eudat.eu/dataset/b5c5d886-27f9-5e6d-8572-0bddac3a44e0); [LINDAT record](https://lindat.mff.cuni.cz/repository/xmlui/handle/11234/1-5198?show=full)
- That licence states that "each of the graphbanks has its own license terms and you (the 'User') are responsible for complying with the license terms applicable to those parts of UMR which you use", warns that "some of the graphbanks permit only non-commercial usage", and names Arapaho-UMR as CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 — [UMR 1.0 licence page (text as returned in search; page itself timed out)](https://lindat.mff.cuni.cz/repository/static/license-umr-1.0.html)
- Guidelines: `guidelines.md` in `umr4nlp/umr-guidelines` is 8,721 lines (≈357 KB); no licence file is stated on the repository page — [UMR guidelines on GitHub](https://github.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/blob/master/guidelines.md)
- Sentence-level inventory (from the guidelines):
  - Aspect lattice (Part 3-3-1), coarse to fine: habitual, imperfective, process, atelic process, perfective, state, activity, endeavor, performance; sub-types reversible state, irreversible state, inherent state, point state, undirected activity, directed activity; semelfactive, undirected endeavor, directed endeavor, incremental accomplishment, nonincremental accomplishment, directed achievement, reversible directed achievement, irreversible directed achievement. Habitual, State, Activity, Endeavor, Performance are the default-granularity values; event nominals default to `process`; "earlier-stage annotation tends to use coarser values" — [UMR guidelines (raw)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
  - `:modstr` has six default values combining strength and polarity: `FullAff`, `PrtAff`, `NeutAff`, `NeutNeg`, `PrtNeg`, `FullNeg`; the strength lattice has Full/Partial/Neutral as its base with coarser `Non-neutral` and `Non-full`; finer values "are used only for languages that grammaticalize the distinction"; `:modpred` links a modal predicate (want, …) to the event it modalises; `:quot` links a reported event to its speech predicate — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
  - `:mode` values: `expressive`, `imperative`, `interrogative` (polar and content questions); `:polarity` flags only overt morphosyntactic negation ("does not necessarily signal semantic negation"; propositional negation lives in the modal dependency) — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
  - `:ref-person` defaults 1st/2nd/3rd, with finer first-person exclusive vs inclusive in non-singular numbers and coarser merges (1st/2nd or 2nd/3rd); `:ref-number` default Singular vs Non-singular, finer dual, trial, paucal, greater plural; one example also uses `Obviative` — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
  - Participant roles: `:ARG0`–`:ARG5` where PropBank-style frame files exist; for languages without frame files, generic roles `:actor`, `:undergoer`, `:theme`, `:recipient`, `:causer`, `:instrument`, `:companion` (annotators may upload FLEx lexicons into the tool); non-participant relations include `:cause`, `:temporal`, `:duration`, `:frequency`, `:place`, `:goal`, `:source`, `:direction`, `:medium`, `:topic`, `:mod`, `:manner`, `:poss`, `:part`, `:degree`, `:ord`, `:range`, `:time`, `:quot`, `:modpred`, `:other-role`, plus `:affectee`, `:reason`, `:condition`, `:concession`, `:purpose` — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
  - Intra-sentence discourse relations (Part 3-1-6 / 3-2-1): `and`; `or` with sub-types `inclusive-disj` / `exclusive-disj` (apprehensive is a sub-type of exclusive disjunction); `additive` with `:temporal` (simultaneous), `:pure-addition`, `:substitute`; `consecutive` with `:purpose`, `:manner` (means), `:cause` or `cause-01`, `have-condition-91` or `:condition`, anterior/posterior `:temporal`; `contrast-91` (and `:subtraction`); `unexpected-co-occurrence-91` with `:ARG1/:ARG2` and sub-types negative circumstantial (`:manner`), `have-concession-91`/`:concession`, `:concessive-condition`; `but-91`; worked examples: "If you touch it, it might explode" = `explode-01 :condition touch-01`; "I grabbed a stick in order to defend myself" = `grab-01 :purpose defend` — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
  - Reported speech: the speech event (`say-01`) takes `:ARG0` speaker and `:ARG1` content; the reported event carries `:quot` pointing to the speech event and its own `:modstr`; the guidelines do not separately define direct vs indirect speech — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
  - Example verbatim (Part 3-2-2-3): `(d/ drive-01 :ARG0 (p/ person :ref-person 3rd :ref-number Singular) :direction (w/ west) :aspect Activity :modstr FullAff)` — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
- Document-level inventory (Part 4): metanodes `ROOT`, `DCT` (document creation time), `PAST_REF`, `PRESENT_REF`, `FUTURE_REF`; temporal relations `:before`, `:after`, `:overlap` (perfectly simultaneous), `:contained` (start and end inside another's duration), `:depends-on`; causal relations are always `:after`; each event is linked to a time expression on its line, else elsewhere, else to a preceding Process with compatible modality, else to a tense metanode; modal dependency hangs events on conceivers (`AUTH`, named conceivers, `NULL_CHARGER`/`NULL_BELIEVER` in examples) with the same six strengths plus `Unsp`; conditionals: protasis `:condition` apodosis at sentence level, conditional events `NeutAff`, "done automatically in the conversion from sentence-level to document-level structure"; purpose clauses: main event `PrtAff` relative to a purpose node; commands `PrtAff`, negative commands `PrtNeg`; reported speech has three modal links (author→reporting event, author→reported event, sayer→reported event); coreference `:same-entity`, `:same-event`, `:subset-of` (earlier mention is head); evidentiality is folded into epistemic strength (direct evidence → full, indirect → partial) — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
- Example document-level block verbatim: `(s/ sentence :temporal ((DCT :overlap s1w) (s1w :after s1v)) :modal ((AUTH :FullAff s1p) …` ; the "Mary said that John might have gone to Santa Fe" example has `(PAST_REF :contained s1s)`, `(s1s :before s1g)`, `(AUTH :FullAff s1s)`, `(AUTH :NeutAff s1p)`, `(s1p :NeutAff s1g)` — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
- Stages: "Modal strength is not annotated at Stage 0. Unspecified links are filled in later through frame files and lexical entries"; Stage 1 builds the full modal dependency structure — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
- Tools: UMR-Writer (EMNLP 2021 demo; 2.0 added a keyboard interface and project workflow) — [Zhao et al. 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-demo.19); AnCast (concept F1, labelled/unlabelled relation F1, ~40 % of Smatch's time) and AnCast++ (document-level: modal, temporal, coreference; public on GitHub) — [AnCast++ 2025](https://preview.aclanthology.org/credits/2025.findings-acl.1008); SETUP, an English text-to-UMR parser (fine-tuned AMR parsers; BiBL best: AnCast 84.3, SMATCH++ 91.0 on UMR 2.0 English; baseline text→AMR→UMR only AnCast 20.5) — [arXiv 2512.07068](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07068v1)
- The AMR-UMR shared "91" rolesets are published as `AMR-UMR-91-rolesets.xml` in the PropBank frames repository, which carries a CC-BY-SA-4.0 licence badge — [propbank-frames](https://github.com/propbank/propbank-frames)

**UCCA**

**DRS / Parallel Meaning Bank**
- The PMB (EACL 2017) holds "over 11 million words in four languages: English, German, Italian, and Dutch", English annotated automatically and manually corrected, projected to word-aligned translations; pipeline: segmentation, CCG parsing, universal semantic tagging, symbolisation, compositional DRT analysis — [arXiv 1702.03964](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03964)
- The texts "are sourced from twelve different corpora from a wide range of genres, including, among others: Tatoeba, News-Commentary (via OPUS), Recognizing Textual Entailment, Sherlock Holmes stories, and the Bible"; 11.3 M tokens in 285,154 documents (2017 release) — [arXiv 1702.03964 (as quoted in search results)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.03964)

**SIL semantic-structure tradition (Beekman–Callow–Kopesec, Larson, SSA series)**
- "Semantic structure analysis (or SSA) is a methodology for systematic description of the intended meaning of natural language, developed by the Summer Institute of Linguistics"; its theoretical base is Beekman, Callow & Kopesec 1981, *The Semantic Structure of Written Communication* (Dallas: SIL) — [Wikipedia stub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_structure_analysis)
- Semantic hierarchy (Larson 1984 pp. 30–31 as summarised by Broad): meaning component → concept → proposition → propositional cluster → semantic paragraph → episode → episode cluster → discourse; without skewing a proposition = clause/simple sentence, a propositional cluster = sentence of more than one clause, a semantic paragraph = paragraph, etc. (Larson 1984 p. 271). Concepts name "a thing, an event, an attribute or a relation". Prominence is natural (built into the relation: "an exhortation is more prominent than the grounds it is paired with, the result is more prominent than the reason") or marked (non-typical clause order, focus clitics) — [Broad 2013, *A semantic structural analysis of logical relations in Eastern Arrernte*, AuSIL](https://ausil.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Broad-logical-relations.pdf)
- Larson's "modest set" of 28 inter-propositional relations (chart adapted from Roberts 1997), as reproduced by Broad: EQUAL natural prominence — chronological: sequential, simultaneous; non-chronological: conjoining, alternation. UNEQUAL natural prominence (support–HEAD) — orientation: orienter–CONTEXT, circumstance–HEAD, introduction–HEAD (opening–BODY, move n–HEAD), chronological step n–GOAL, occasion–OUTCOME; clarification: equivalent (restatement–HEAD), GENERIC–specific n, generic–SPECIFIC, contraction/amplification–HEAD, comparison–HEAD, illustration–HEAD, manner–HEAD, contrast–HEAD; logical: reason–RESULT, means–RESULT, MEANS–purpose, condition–CONSEQUENCE, concession–CONTRAEXPECTATION, grounds–CONCLUSION, grounds–EXHORTATION; associative: comment–HEAD, parenthesis–HEAD; concept–communication-unit relations: identification, description. Five relations (sequential, simultaneous, conjoining, alternation, …) are multilateral; the rest bilateral; linear order is grammatical, the relation itself unordered; "In the majority of languages there is not a one-to-one matching between the semantic inter-propositional relations and the grammatical sentence types and/or conjunctions" — [Broad 2013](https://ausil.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Broad-logical-relations.pdf)
- Beekman & Callow define the HEAD as the "development" proposition and classify support as clarifying, arguing or orienting — [Broad 2013](https://ausil.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Broad-logical-relations.pdf)
- SSA volumes exist in print from SIL, e.g. John Callow, *A Semantic Structure Analysis of Colossians* (SIL 1983, 246 pp.; 2nd ed. in the "Semantic and Structural Analysis Series") and Sherman & Tuggy, *A semantic and structural analysis of the Johannine epistles* (SIL 1994); the series is described as "analytical commentaries on the Greek text of New Testament books … to help translators"; a *2 Thessalonians* volume is in print (ISBN 9781556711114) — [library record](https://library.aiu.ac.ke/bib/19170); [Colossians 2nd ed.](https://www.boekhandeldominicanen.nl/a/john-callow/a-semantic-and-structural-analysis-of-colossians--2nd-edition/9781556711305); [2 Thessalonians](https://www.regulatorbookshop.com/book/9781556711114)
- Larson, *Meaning-Based Translation: A Guide to Cross-Language Equivalence*, University Press of America 1984 (bibliography pp. 507–521) — [catalogue record](https://lib.pnu.ac.ir/inventory/4/43373.htm)

**TBTA / The Translator's Assistant / Linguist's Assistant (Allman, Beale)**
- The 2005 MT Summit paper (Beale, Nirenburg, McShane, Allman) describes converting the Bible text manually into a controlled English, analysing it semi-automatically (word sense, POS, root, syntactic dependencies with case roles) into Text Meaning Representations (TMRs) "made up of word senses … connected by semantic relations", with attributes and an ontology "specially constructed for the Biblical domain"; "a detailed analysis of time and aspect are also part of the semi-automatic analysis process" — [Beale et al. 2005, MT Summit X](https://aclanthology.org/2005.mtsummit-papers.9/)
- Controlled-language rules (same paper, section 2.2): no possessive 's, only "of", whose sense must be chosen from "22 possible semantic relations … (for example, ownership, kinship, made-of, etc.)"; pronouns only where semantically unambiguous, with senses distinguishing "number (sing, dual, trial and pl) and exclusivity"; imperatives and questions marked in the text by `(imp)`, `(yn-ques)`, `(ques)` with the sentence entered in declarative form; "Every event is propositionalized"; most figurative language eliminated ("metonymy, synecdoche, euphemisms and idioms, and metaphors are converted to similes and the point of similarity is supplied"); no reduced relatives. Status 2005: "over 3000 verses" analysed; TBTA tested for English, Korean, Jula and Kewa ("a clause chaining language spoken in Papua New Guinea"); a Grammar Development module of about 300 propositions "illustrates a variety of verbal aspects and moods, relative clauses formed on a variety of semantic roles, patient propositions … different types of adverbial clauses, different types of questions" — [Beale et al. 2005 (PDF text)](https://aclanthology.org/2005.mtsummit-papers.9.pdf)
- Allman's 2010 UTA dissertation: TTA's four components are ontology (Natural Semantic Metalanguage principles), semantic representations ("a controlled, English influenced metalanguage augmented by a feature system … designed to accommodate a very wide variety of target languages"), transfer grammar (Functional-Typological grammar, Role and Reference Grammar) and synthesizing grammar; semantic representations were written for 105 chapters; 50 chapters generated in Korean, all 105 in English; translators' "productivity is typically quadrupled without any loss of quality" — [Allman 2010, UTA](https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/linguistics_tesol_dissertations/38)
- Linguist's Assistant (INLG 2012, Allman, Beale, Denton) is the same generator; the Tagalog paper lists tests in English, Korean, Kewa, Jula and prototypes for Spanish, Urdu, North Tanna, Angas, Chinantec; translators "tripled their productivity" — [INLG 2012](https://aclanthology.org/W12-1510/); [Tagalog paper](https://www.national-u.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Linguist’s-Assistant-Gleaning-a-Tagalog-Lexicon-and-Grammar-from-a-Small-Lightly-Annotated-Corpus.pdf)
- Today the organisation is "All the Word" (formerly The Bible Translator's Assistant Inc.; Tod J. Allman president); its site lists translations in Tagalog, Ayta Mag-Indi, Botolan Sambal, English, Korean, Kewa, Jula and work in India, Indonesia, Kenya, PNG, the Philippines, Russia, SE Asia, Vanuatu, with "Software", "Documentation" and "AI Experiments" pages — [GuideStar](https://www.guidestar.org/profile/27-2697222); [alltheword.org](https://alltheword.org/)
- Wycliffe Canada (2024): TBTA "combine[s] grammar analysis of a language with templates of books of the Bible" to create a first draft "with both exegetical and linguistic notes"; translators "are helping develop and encode the templates of all 66 books"; the team was "analyzing seven different regional and national languages" and tested TBTA alongside FLExTrans; communities report time/cost cut "up to 40%" (self-reported) — [Wycliffe Canada update 2024](https://wycliffe.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2024-SUMMER-TBTA-Project-Update.pdf); [wycliffe.ca](https://wycliffe.ca/?p=16164)

## Key question 1 — What is the minimal clause-level meaning inventory that generation into typologically diverse languages needs?

### Takeaway
Across the four traditions that have actually generated or translated into typologically diverse languages (TBTA, SIL's SSA/Larson, UMR's low-resource design, Levinsohn's discourse courses), the common core is: one proposition per event with semantic roles and senses; aspect and modal strength as lattices that can stay coarse; polarity; speech act (imperative, question, exhortation strength); an inter-clause relation from a closed list (~25–30) with the prominent member marked; person/number including clusivity, dual/trial/paucal; participant identity across clauses; quotation nesting with speaker and addressee; temporal order of events; and explicit implicit information. Honorifics and evidentiality are not in any inventory found except UMR's folding of evidentiality into modal strength.

### Cited Findings
- UMR's design for low-resource languages uses lattices: Full/Partial/Neutral modal strength with coarser (`Non-neutral`, `Non-full`) and finer values "used only for languages that grammaticalize the distinction"; aspect from coarse `imperfective`/`perfective`/`state`/`process` to fine `semelfactive`, `directed achievement`…; person 1st/2nd/3rd with inclusive/exclusive finer; number Singular/Non-singular with dual, trial, paucal, greater plural finer; evidentiality folded into epistemic support (direct → full, indirect → partial) — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
- UMR's generic roles for languages without frame files: `:actor`, `:undergoer`, `:theme`, `:recipient`, `:causer`, `:instrument`, `:companion` — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
- TBTA's controlled language forces exactly the decisions a target language may need: pronoun senses by "number (sing, dual, trial and pl) and exclusivity"; `(imp)`, `(yn-ques)`, `(ques)` marks; one proposition per event; 22 senses of "of"; figurative language resolved and "the point of similarity is supplied"; time and aspect analysed; its grammar-development module covers "verbal aspects and moods, relative clauses formed on a variety of semantic roles, patient propositions (object complements) … different types of adverbial clauses, different types of questions" — [Beale et al. 2005](https://aclanthology.org/2005.mtsummit-papers.9.pdf)
- TBTA's restructuring grammar does "case frame adjustments … generate grammatical relations from semantic roles, build clause chains with medial and final verbs" (the Kewa case), e.g. Korean lacks PREVENT so "Mary prevented John from reading the book" is restructured to "Because of Mary, John was unable to read the book" — [Beale et al. 2005](https://aclanthology.org/2005.mtsummit-papers.9.pdf)
- Larson's inventory: 28 inter-propositional relations, binary ones with a HEAD ("natural prominence"), five multilateral; relations are unordered, order is grammar; the semantic unit hierarchy from meaning component to discourse; skewing between semantic and grammatical units is expected (Larson 1984 p. 271) — [Broad 2013](https://ausil.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Broad-logical-relations.pdf)
- Broad's Arrernte study shows why the relation must be separated from its marking: BECAUSE-type relations are expressed by `-iperre` 'after', dative `-ke`, `-warte` 'since', ablative `-nge`, switch reference and juxtaposition, and same- vs different-subject purpose clauses differ — [Broad 2013](https://ausil.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Broad-logical-relations.pdf)
- Levinsohn's course topics for a new language: constituent order, backgrounding and highlighting, connectives, reporting conversation, participant reference (narrative); instruction vs persuasion, prominence, "the relative strength of forms of exhortation", boundary features (non-narrative) — [koine-greek.com](https://koine-greek.com/2023/01/11/levinsohns-discourse-analysis-materials/)
- Runge's framework treats connectives as marked/unmarked for constraints (development, forward-pointing…), redundant quotative frames as speaker/hearer/topic shifts, and distinguishes emphasis, prominence and focus — [WLS review](https://www.wisluthsem.org/discourse/)
- UMR's document-level temporal rules link every event to a time expression or a preceding event or a tense metanode; causal relations are `:after`; `:overlap` vs `:contained` — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
- UCCA's refined implicit-argument annotation exists as a separate layer (paper "Refining Implicit Argument Annotation for UCCA") — [arXiv 2005.12889](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.12889)

### Inferences
- Minimal per-clause record (my synthesis): `event(sense)`; `roles` (actor/undergoer/theme/recipient + place/goal/source/instrument/companion); `aspect` (coarse: state | process | perfective | imperfective | habitual; finer optional); `modstr` (FullAff/PrtAff/NeutAff/NeutNeg/PrtNeg/FullNeg); `polarity`; `mode` (declarative | imperative | interrogative(polar/content) | expressive) plus exhortation strength where relevant; `relation` to parent clause (Larson/UMR closed list) with `head` (which side is prominent); `temporal` (before/after/overlap previous event, or absolute); `speech` (quotation id, level, speaker, addressee, direct/indirect); `participants` with ids and person/number/clusivity and discourse status (new / given / reactivated); `implicit` (supplied participants or information); `prominence` (natural / marked, foreground / background).
- The Kuni (clause-chaining, SOV) and Zinza (Bantu) targets need in particular: same/different-subject information across chained clauses (derivable from `subjref`), tense/aspect per event (needs interpretation of Hebrew forms), clusivity (never in the source; needs addressee information from the speaker layer and the narrative context), and noun-class/agreement (a lexical property of the target, not of the layer).
- Honorific information (speaker–addressee social rank) is in no inventory found; it would have to be derived from the speaker layer's speaker/addressee plus a character table (Glyssen character names are already in the speaker layer).

### Gaps
- No source was found that gives an empirically validated "minimal" inventory; the lists above are the unions of what the schemes record. Larson's own chapter text (communication relations, implicit information, skewing) was only available second-hand through Broad 2013; the book itself was not accessible online.
- TBTA's full feature system (the dissertation's feature tables for nouns, verbs and clauses) was not retrievable: the UTA repository failed on certificate, the abstract page gives no feature list, and the 2012 INLG abstract page has no abstract text.
- Nothing was found on honorific or evidential marking in any Bible-oriented scheme.

## Key question 2 — Which parts can a script derive from MACULA (plus the speaker layer), and which need human or LLM annotation?

### Takeaway
From MACULA a script can derive, per clause: the predicate lemma and its SDBH/Louw–Nida sense, A0/A1 and syntactic roles, participant ids and subject reference, the morphological TAM (Greek tense/voice/mood; Hebrew verb type, stem, person/number/gender), overt negation, interrogative and vocative particles, the subordinator/conjunction and the clause's attachment inside the verse, and (with the speaker layer) quotation nesting and speaker. It cannot give clause relations across verses, Hebrew aspect/tense interpretation, pragmatic speech acts beyond mood, implicit participants, prominence, inclusive/exclusive, addressees, or the discourse relation behind a bare וְ/καί — these are exactly UMR's Stage 1 and SSA's analyst decisions.

### Cited Findings
- MACULA Hebrew word `type` values seen in Ruth 1 (local inspection): wayyiqtol 34, qatal 19, yiqtol 17, infinitive construct 12, imperative 8, participle active 3, jussive 3, negative 3; Greek verbs carry `tense`, `voice`, `mood`, `person`, `number` (e.g. `ἀπόληται mood=subjunctive tense=aorist voice=middle`). (Local inspection of `download/macula-hebrew/47db250bd55d/08-Rut-001-lowfat.xml` and `download/macula-greek/8423afe47b9e/04-john.xml`, 2026-10-22.)
- MACULA `frame` strings give A0/A1 by word id, including for infinitives and participles (`עָזְבֵ frame="A0:080010160021; A1:080010160063;" subjref=080010160021`); unresolved generic subjects appear as `A0:n00000000000` (John 3:16 `πιστεύων`). (Local inspection.)
- The clause layout's rules show what MACULA lacks: "MACULA has no clause-to-clause relations above the verse"; the text-level hierarchy is derived "from MACULA's features: verb forms, the clause's first words, verbs of saying, subordinators, person, and the MACULA tree" — [hebrew-clause-layout](https://huggingface.co/datasets/bcv-commons/hebrew-clause-layout)
- BHSA's `vt` documentation itself states "The meanings/functions of these tenses should be clarified" — aspect/tense is not an annotated interpretation even in BHSA — [BHSA vt](https://etcbc.github.io/bhsa/features/vt/)
- UMR's `:polarity` "flags morphosyntactic negation markers … does not necessarily signal semantic negation"; semantic negation is a modal-dependency decision — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
- UMR Stage 0 omits modal strength; frame files and lexical entries fill many links automatically later; conditionals' modal structure is "done automatically in the conversion from sentence-level to document-level structure" — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
- The best text-to-UMR parser is fine-tuned (not an LLM) and reaches AnCast 84 / SMATCH++ 91 on English UMR 2.0, while converting AMR output to UMR by rule gives only AnCast ≈20 — [SETUP](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07068v1)
- The speaker layer's Hebrew refinements are rule-based (inquit edges, prefix/particle edges, messenger formulas, commanded messages, first/second-person runs), with nine translations' quotation marks as votes and hand corrections/reviews for doubtful passages (188 reviewed changes) — [hebrew-speaker-layer](https://huggingface.co/datasets/bcv-commons/hebrew-speaker-layer)
- SSA/Larson: relations are not read off conjunctions ("not a one-to-one matching between the semantic inter-propositional relations and the grammatical sentence types and/or conjunctions"); implicit relations and constituents are a named analysis step (Broad ch. 3.2) — [Broad 2013](https://ausil.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Broad-logical-relations.pdf)
- TBTA's analysis relies on a single expert document author checking word senses, PP attachment and pronoun antecedents, and on removing figurative language by hand — [Beale et al. 2005](https://aclanthology.org/2005.mtsummit-papers.9.pdf)

### Inferences
- Derivable by script (deterministic from MACULA attributes): clause id and span; predicate lemma + sense code; `role` s/o/io/adv groups and `frame` A0/A1; `subjref`/`participantref`/`referent` chains (→ participant ids, same-subject flags); morphological TAM; overt negation (`אַל`, `לֹא`, `οὐ`, `μή`); mood-based mode (imperative/jussive/cohortative → imperative; interrogative particle/pronoun → interrogative; vocative case); subordinator lemma and `rule` (`sub-CL`, `relCL`, `PrepCL` infinitive) → a first-pass relation (ἵνα/לְמַעַן → purpose; ὥστε → result; εἰ/אִם/כִּי+protasis → condition; ὅτι/כִּי after perception → content; γάר → ground; relative → restriction); quotation id/level/speaker (speaker layer, Hebrew; Clear's spans for Greek); verse-internal attachment and, through the clause layout, a rule-derived parent across verses with a `relation`.
- Needs LLM/human (UMR Stage 1 / SSA analyst work): aspect value of each Hebrew form (wayyiqtol narrative perfective vs qatal state/anterior vs yiqtol future/habitual/modal; participle progressive); modal strength of yiqtol/jussive/subjunctive; speech act beyond mood (plea vs prohibition in Ruth 1:16's אַל־תִּפְגְּעִי; rhetorical questions); the relation behind bare וְ/καί and asyndeton (sequence, simultaneity, result, contrast); which clause is HEAD; implicit participants and addressees (needed for clusivity and honorifics); the referent of generic participles (`A0:n00000000000`); idiom/metaphor resolution; `:same-event` and `:subset-of` coreference; temporal ordering where verse order is not event order (flashbacks, qatal after wayyiqtol).
- A pragmatic split: ship the deterministic layer as data (CC BY 4.0 derived from MACULA CC BY; speaker layer CC BY; clause layout CC BY-SA, so a published combined file is CC BY-SA unless the clause-layout columns are kept separate), and let an agent fill a short list of Stage-1 fields per clause with a confidence, stored as its own layer.

### Gaps
- No measurement exists of how accurately rules can assign UMR-style relations from Greek/Hebrew subordinators; the clause layout's step agreement with BHSA (about 86 % over the Tanakh per the project's private notes) is the only related figure and it measures indentation, not relation labels.
- Clear's "semantic roles" dataset (beyond A0/A1 in `frame`) and its participant-referent coverage were not documented on the README; coverage figures (how many verbs have frames) were not computed here.

## Key question 4 — How do UMR's document-level relations compare with what the Hebrew speaker layer and clause layout give?

### Takeaway
UMR's document level has three graphs — temporal, modal, coreference — over events and entities. The speaker layer covers the quotation part of UMR's modal graph (who reports what, nesting, speaker) with far more detail than UMR's `:quot` chain; the clause layout covers inter-clause attachment with a `relation` and depth, which corresponds to UMR's intra-sentence discourse relations extended across verses, but neither gives event-time ordering, modal strength or entity/event coreference across clauses, which UMR requires.

### Cited Findings
- UMR document level: temporal (`:before`, `:after`, `:overlap`, `:contained`, `:depends-on`, metanodes DCT/PAST_REF/…), modal (conceivers AUTH etc. with six strengths + `Unsp`; reported speech links author→reporting event, author→reported event, sayer→reported event), coreference (`:same-entity`, `:same-event`, `:subset-of`) — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
- Speaker layer: per segment `quotation`, `level`, `speaker`, `frame`; per quotation `parent`, `speaker`, `speaker_hebrew`, `divine`, `inquit`, `first`/`last` word keys; "Quotations inside quotations" from Hebrew structure, commanded messages and translations' votes; two readings of the prophets (frame as field vs as level) — [hebrew-speaker-layer](https://huggingface.co/datasets/bcv-commons/hebrew-speaker-layer)
- Clause layout: `discourse_parent` and `relation` for every clause segment; flat relations (`continuation`, `coordination`, `asyndetic`, `parallel`…) vs level-adding ones (speech, subordinate, embedded); conditionals laid out with the protasis one level in and the apodosis level with it; `text_depth`, `text_depth_law`, `tree_depth`, `depth` — [hebrew-clause-layout](https://huggingface.co/datasets/bcv-commons/hebrew-clause-layout)
- MACULA participant references (`subjref`, `participantref`, `referent`) point to word ids of antecedents, i.e. entity coreference within and across verses at word level (local inspection: Ruth 1:16 `participantref=080010140072` on every "you" pointing back to Ruth 1:14's Ruth). (Local inspection.)
- BHSA `txt` gives narrative/quotation/discursive nesting strings per clause (e.g. `QND`) — [BHSA txt](https://etcbc.github.io/bhsa/features/txt/)

### Inferences
- Mapping: speaker layer → UMR `:quot` edges plus the sayer conceiver; `level` → nesting depth of conceivers; `speaker` → conceiver identity (UMR has no addressee field either; neither does the speaker layer, which is a gap for clusivity/honorifics). Clause layout `relation` → UMR `:purpose/:condition/:cause/…` where the rule names a subordinator, and `and`/`consecutive` for chain links; `discourse_parent` across verses → something UMR has no name for (UMR's discourse relations are sentence-internal; across sentences it only has temporal/modal/coref), so the layout is richer than UMR there.
- Missing relative to UMR: event-time ordering (`:before/:after/:overlap`) — derivable as a default from verse order and wayyiqtol chains, but flashbacks and simultaneity need judgement; modal strength per event (rule defaults: narrative wayyiqtol FullAff, imperatives PrtAff, yiqtol in speech NeutAff/PrtAff, conditionals NeutAff — these follow UMR's own defaults for commands and conditionals); `:same-event` (repeated reports, e.g. Genesis 24's retelling) and `:subset-of`.
- Practical consequence: the "light layer" can emit a UMR-compatible document block with `:quot`/modal chains from the speaker layer and default temporal links from clause order, leaving `:same-event`, non-default modal strengths and non-sequential temporal links as explicit `Unsp` for an agent.

### Gaps
- No published comparison of the speaker layer with UMR-style modal annotation exists; the layer's own accuracy figures (in the project's private notes) are against BHSA `txt`, not against a UMR gold standard.
- UMR's treatment of addressees (vocatives, `:vocative`) was not located in the readable parts of the guidelines.

## Key question 5 — Which notation is most compact and still machine-parseable? Any token-count comparisons?

### Takeaway
No published token-count comparison between PENMAN, triples and JSON for meaning representations was found. PENMAN (UMR/AMR) is a compact human-readable graph notation with mature parsers; UMR graphs are long because every event carries `:aspect` and `:modstr` and every entity `:ref-person`/`:ref-number`. For a per-clause layer with mostly flat attributes, a TSV row per clause is the cheapest machine-parseable form; JSON with quoted keys is the most expensive in tokens because every quote, colon and brace is a token. A rough local count on John 3:16 (below) gives: Greek text ≈47 tokens, controlled English ≈54, SSA display ≈63, TSV light layer ≈128, UMR sentence+document graphs ≈263, JSON-lines light layer ≈437.

### Cited Findings
- AMR/UMR are written "as lisp-like source code … readable by people and traversable by machines" (PENMAN) — [LDC2020T02 README](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/docs/LDC2020T02/README.txt); UMR adds a document-level block per sentence in the same notation — [UMR guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umr4nlp/umr-guidelines/refs/heads/master/guidelines.md)
- PMB DRS clause format: "sets of clauses, one per line", each clause a box variable, an operator/role/concept and arguments; SBN and box notations are alternatives — [RikVN/DRS_parsing](https://github.com/RikVN/DRS_parsing)
- UCCA passages are XML files; the format is documented in a FORMAT.md — [UCCA English-Wiki](https://github.com/UniversalConceptualCognitiveAnnotation/UCCA_English-Wiki)
- OpenText and MACULA use inline XML; MACULA also ships TSV "without tree structure" — [macula-greek README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Clear-Bible/macula-greek/main/README.md); [OpenText v1.0](https://github.com/OpenText-org/GNT_annotation_v1.0)
- The clause layout ships JSONL per chapter, Parquet per book and indented plain text — [hebrew-clause-layout](https://huggingface.co/datasets/bcv-commons/hebrew-clause-layout)
- The SETUP paper notes that long UMR graphs make seq2seq output drop the final parenthesis — a practical fragility of PENMAN for generation — [SETUP](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07068v1)
- SSA displays are indented English propositions with relation labels (support–HEAD), one proposition per line — [Broad 2013](https://ausil.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Broad-logical-relations.pdf); TBTA's input is controlled English with inline marks like `(imp)` — [Beale et al. 2005](https://aclanthology.org/2005.mtsummit-papers.9.pdf)

- TSV (or Parquet) per clause is the right storage and prompt format for the light layer: flat, one record per clause, diffable, and the cheapest in tokens; JSON lines cost more than twice as many tokens for the same content because of the quoting, so JSON should be the API form, not the prompt form. PENMAN is worth emitting only where a graph is needed (embedded clauses as arguments, coreference); UMR labels can be used as the controlled vocabulary inside the TSV so the layer stays "UMR-compatible" without PENMAN's nesting. For reading by an agent, the controlled-English rendering is five to eight times cheaper than any structured form and is what TBTA's generation record supports.
- For agents, the controlled-English rendering (d) is the cheapest to read and closest to what TBTA proved works for generation, so a script should be able to print (d) from (a)+(Stage-1 fields).

### Gaps
- No published token counts for PENMAN vs triples vs JSON were found; the table above is a local approximation (see counting note) rather than a cited figure.
- No UMR-to-text generation system or evaluation was found; AMR-to-text generation exists in general NLP but was not surveyed here, and nothing Bible-specific was found.
