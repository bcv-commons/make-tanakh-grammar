# The Bible Translator's Assistant (TBTA / TTA / Linguist's Assistant / All the Word), state as of 2026-10

Research notes, 2026-10-22. Scope: TBTA's semantic representation, generator, coverage, licence, organisation, and what a per-clause universal specification project could reuse. Older facts are dated. Primary sources were read in full where possible (dissertation, papers, the GitHub data export's README and example JSON, a third-party 2025 hackathon repository that reverse-engineered TBTA's 2025 documentation). Several official pages (tbta.info, tbta.bible, btconference.org 2023 proceedings, wycliffe.org blog posts, Wycliffe Canada's 2024 PDF update) could not be fetched from this environment; what is known of them comes from search snippets and is marked as such.

Naming: the same system appears as **TTA** (The Translator's Assistant, Allman's 2010 dissertation), **LA** (Linguist's Assistant, the language-documentation framing, 2011–2014 papers), **TBTA** (The Bible Translator's Assistant, the Bible deployment, 2005 onward), **ATW** (All the Word, the non-profit, formerly "The Bible Translator's Assistant Inc"), and in 2025 a tools.bible listing calls it **TaBiThA** ("formerly TBTA"). Wycliffe Canada runs a separate, partnered TBTA development and deployment effort (project lead David Duncan).

## Q1. What exactly is in a TBTA semantic representation (features, ontology, clause structure, encoding format, examples)?

### Takeaway
A TBTA semantic representation is a controlled, English-based "rich interlingua": each verse is a sequence of clauses (`{}`), subclauses (`[]`) and phrases (`()`) whose words are senses from a closed ontology of semantically simple concepts (NSM primitives + Longman defining vocabulary, each sense narrowly defined, e.g. 25 senses of BE), and every word, phrase and clause carries a fixed-position single-character feature string: nouns ~10 features (number incl. dual/trial/paucal, participant tracking, person incl. clusivity, proximity, surface realization, participant status, coreference index), verbs ~9 (a 25-value deictic Time, aspect, 11-value mood/potential/obligation, reflexivity, 4-value polarity, plus target TAM slots), noun phrases (semantic role, implicit-information type, relativized, sequence), and clauses ~18 (type, illocutionary force, topic, speaker/listener identity, attitude, ages, discourse genre, Longacre notional-structure slot, salience band, location-in-discourse incl. footnote types, implicit-information type, alternative analysis, vocabulary alternates, rhetorical-question type). It is meaning-based in the Larson/Longacre tradition: no pronouns, one event per proposition, implicit information made explicit and flagged, figurative language mostly removed, "the reason for the form" encoded (e.g. a pluperfect becomes a Flashback salience value).

### Cited Findings

**Architecture and theoretical basis**
- Four core components: "1) the ontology, 2) the semantic representations, 3) the transfer grammar, and 4) the synthesizing grammar. This system's ontology was developed using the foundational principles of Natural Semantic Metalanguage theory. TTA's semantic representations are comprised of a controlled, English influenced metalanguage augmented by a feature system which was designed to accommodate a very wide variety of target languages. TTA's transfer grammar incorporates many concepts from Functional-Typological grammar as well as Role and Reference grammar. The synthesizing grammar ... most closely resembles the transformational-generative model. The meaning-based theory of translation underlies the TTA system." — [Allman 2010 dissertation, abstract](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf) (also [UTA MavMatrix record](https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/linguistics_tesol_dissertations/38), PhD, University of Texas at Arlington, December 2010, advisor Jerold Edmondson).
- Semantic representations "consist of concepts, structures, and features. The concepts ... are all semantically simple ... The structures permitted ... are a small restricted set of English-like sentence structures. The feature system ... includes semantic, syntactic, and discourse information. The feature values have been gleaned from a wide variety of diverse languages." — [Allman, Beale & Denton, INLG 2012](https://aclanthology.org/W12-1510.pdf), §3.
- The representation is called the "rich interlingua" approach, likened to Wilks' "Common Sense Semantics". — [Dissertation §3.3.3.9](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf)
- The feature system draws on Longacre for Discourse Genre, Notional Structure Schema and Salience Band (Longacre 1996:10, 28, 36), Participant Tracking (Longacre 1995:702; Prince 1981:230) and Participant Status (Longacre 1995:701; Bartsch 1995:47); the dissertation cites Larson 1984 (e.g. Tzeltal "carry" verbs, p. 89) and Comrie/Dahl/Givón typology for tense, aspect, relative clauses and complements. — [Dissertation §1.3.1–1.3.2](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf)
- "TTA's semantic analysis ... attempts to identify the reasons why surface structures in the source documents have the forms that they do ... if a pluperfect verb form occurs, there are two possible reasons: 1) the author is using flashback ... or 2) ... a counterfactual construction ... Therefore, when a pluperfect occurs in a source document, the reason for its use is determined and encoded in the semantic representation. TTA's semantic analysis also includes markers to indicate the beginning of each episode, the beginning of each scene, a variety of nominal-nominal relationships, etc." — [Dissertation §1.3.1](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf)

**Ontology (concept inventory)**
- "TTA's ontology is a simple taxonomy ... consists primarily of semantically simple English lexemes." Four semantic complexity levels: 1) NSM primitives, 2) semantic molecules = "words that are in Longman's defining vocabulary" (~2000 words), 3) explicated complex concepts with an associated Complex Concept Insertion rule (e.g. veterinarian = "doctor that treats sick animals"; shepherd = "man that takes care of sheep"), 4) inexplicable concepts (proper names, numbers, relationship markers, particles). Seven semantic categories: Objects, Events, Object Attributes, Event Attributes, Relations, Conjunctions, Particles; "at the present time the concepts in each category are in simple lists; they are not subcategorized or in any type of hierarchical relationship." — [Dissertation §3.3.1](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf); the 2014 paper gives a five-level version (adds "complex concepts that don't yet have an insertion rule") and says NSM has "approximately 65 semantically simple concepts". — [Allman, Beale & Denton, PCJ 2014](https://alltheword.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Philippine-Computing-Journal-2014.pdf)
- Senses are narrow and construction-bound: "TTA's ontology includes twenty-five distinct senses of BE. ... BE-D ... attributive (John is tall), BE-E ... existential, BE-F ... locative, BE-M ... social role (John is a teacher)"; SAY-A (direct quote, patient proposition, optional destination), SAY-B (patient object phrase), SAY-C (indirect speech), SAY-D (a law/command/message says). Complex-concept alternates are written inline as "X angrily criticize/scold Y". — [Dissertation §1.3.1, §3.3.1.3](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf)
- In the exported data, lexical senses appear as a letter after the concept (`Ruth` sense A; `thing` senses A/B; `grain/Bbarley` = simple/complex pair where the complex word carries its sense letter). — [tbta_db_export README](https://github.com/AllTheWord/tbta_db_export/blob/main/README.md)
- A third-party restructuring of the current databases counts "~5.7k concepts" (`concepts.sqlite`: id, stem, sense, part_of_speech, gloss, brief_gloss, occurrences, strongs_mappings) and "~500k nodes" over "~17.5k verses" in `verses.sqlite` (the verse count is larger than the 11,649 exported in 2023; the hackathon's source was "the original TBTA files, Bible.sqlite and Ontology.sqlite", not tracked in git). — [tbta-ai-framework databases/README.md](https://github.com/authenticwalk/tbta-ai-framework/blob/main/databases/README.md)
- 2005 design: Text Meaning Representations (TMR) "made up of word senses (such as wash-a ...) connected by semantic relations ... All of the word senses, relations and attributes are defined in our ontology (which was specially constructed for the Biblical domain)"; "of" must be resolved to one of "22 possible semantic relations (ownership, kinship, made-of, etc.)". — [Beale, Nirenburg, McShane & Allman, MT Summit 2005](https://aclanthology.org/2005.mtsummit-papers.9.pdf)

**The controlled language (the "Phase 1" authoring text)**
- 2005 rules: no possessive 's (use "of" with a chosen relation sense); pronouns only where semantically unambiguous, with senses distinguishing number (sing, dual, trial, pl) and exclusivity; imperatives and questions marked `(imp)`, `(yn-ques)`, `(ques)` on a declarative-form sentence with the questioned constituent replaced by who/where; "Every event is propositionalized"; "We eliminate most figurative language, except when it has theological implications ... metaphors are converted to similes and the point of similarity is supplied"; KANT-style restrictions such as no reduced relative clauses. — [MT Summit 2005 §2.2](https://aclanthology.org/2005.mtsummit-papers.9.pdf)
- 2025 practice (TBTA's own "Analysis Conventions"/notation document as mirrored by a hackathon repo): relative time in relative and patient clauses; no Discourse time inside quotes; English perfect is analysed as the "flashback" feature; `_paragraph`/`(paragraph)`, `(comment-begin)`/`(comment-end)`, `(poetry-begin)`/`(poetry-end)`, `(blank-line)`; quotes bracketed as patient clauses `John said, ["Mary read that book"]`. — [notation.md (mirrored TBTA conventions)](https://github.com/authenticwalk/tbta-ai-framework/blob/main/experiments/encoding_phases/phase1-encoding/reference/notation.md)
- Hackathon-derived encoder rules that reflect TBTA's linter: 3rd-person pronouns replaced by nouns; 1st/2nd pronouns carry referents `I(Paul)`, `you(people)`, `we(Peter)_excl`; one verb per clause; vocabulary levels L0–L4 with `simple/complex` pairs (`serve/worship`, `grain/barley`, `family/clan`) and `(complex) X. (simple) Y.` alternates; brackets for relative `[who/that...]`, purpose `[in order to...]`, temporal, conditional and patient clauses; implicit information `<<info>>` (regular) vs `<info>` (necessary); passives `by X _implicitActiveAgent`; literal/dynamic alternates `(literal) ... (dynamic) ...`; the TBTA checker API reports per-token messages, `lookup_results` with vocabulary `level`/`gloss`/`pairing`, and verb `case_frame` with `required_roles`/`missing_arguments`. — [CODEX-PROMPT.md](https://github.com/authenticwalk/tbta-ai-framework/blob/main/prompts/predict-phase1/CODEX-PROMPT.md), [fine-tune README](https://github.com/authenticwalk/tbta-ai-framework/blob/main/experiments/fine-tune-llm/README.md)

**Structures (propositions)**
- Clause/proposition types: Independent, Coordinate Independent, Restrictive Thing Modifier (relative), Descriptive Thing Modifier (relative), Event Modifier (adverbial), Agent (subject complement), Patient (object complement), Attributive Patient, Closing Quotation Frame. Dissertation §3.3.3 covers event-modifying, object-modifying, agent, patient, attributive-patient propositions, closing quotation frames, object-object relationships, comparisons, and discourse. — [tbta_db_export README](https://github.com/AllTheWord/tbta_db_export/blob/main/README.md); [Dissertation §3.3.3 headings](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf)
- Discourse conventions: "Every episode begins with a semantic marker labeled 'Begin Episode' ... Every scene within an episode begins with ... 'Begin Scene'"; closing quotation propositions are embedded in the final proposition of multi-proposition quotes and removed by rule for languages like English; "There are no pronouns in the semantic representations; language specific rules must generate all the pronouns"; "each proposition contains only one event", rules may later merge propositions; spatial relations are sense-specific (ON-B = on the surface) and corrected by collocation rules ("sand on eye" → "sand in eye" in English). Example generated English for Infected Eye 1:2: "One day a girl named Melissa was sitting outside her house. But Melissa was not happy because her eyes were very sore. She thought that some sand was in her eyes. So she called a friend named Janet and said to her, 'Please look at my eyes. Is some sand in my eyes?'" — [Dissertation §3.3.3.9](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf)
- After the Jula test the representations were revised: "Conjunctions were added to the beginning of many propositions"; "The Proximity feature on objects was used much more extensively" (Contextually Near with Focus → this, Contextually Near → that, as a substitute for the definite article in languages without one); "Relational phrases that modify objects were converted to object modifying propositions" (the man in that house → the man who is in that house), which removed the need for transfer rules in Kewa and Korean. — [Dissertation §6.5](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf)

### Inferences
- The clause-level record (type + illocutionary force + speaker/listener/attitude/age + genre + notional slot + salience + implicit-info type + alternatives) is almost exactly the "(A) each source clause in universal terms" list the bsb-add-books spec wants, with two differences: TBTA's clause relations are coded only as the subclause's grammatical type (relative/adverbial/complement) plus an inline conjunction concept (because, in-order-to, when…), not as a typed discourse relation on every clause; and participant roles are a 9-value macro-role set (agent-like, patient-like, state, source, destination, instrument, addressee, beneficiary) rather than verb-specific frames (frames exist on the ontology side as case frames per sense).
- Verb features are deliberately deictic and semantic (Time relative to speech/discourse, aspect as Aktionsart-like phases, mood as potential/obligation strength) rather than morphological; polarity is on both verb and noun; "target TAM" slots exist for per-language overrides.
- Several values are schema-only: the hackathon's counts over 171,876 noun annotations show Restaging, Integration and Exiting at 0 and Offstage at 1 (Routine 73%, Generic 14%, Frame Inferable 7.5%, First Mention 5.4%); Speech Style has a single value. The inventory is wider than the data.

### Gaps
- The dissertation's §3.3.2 (pp. 73–94) was not read line by line; the inventory above is from the export README and Appendix E.
- No official document defines "He1" or the full 2025 "Analysis Conventions"; the only copies seen are mirrored in a third-party repo.
- The size of the current ontology is known only from a third-party restructuring (~5.7k concepts); no official count found.

## Q2. How does the generator work, what grammar does a linguist write, how much work per language?

### Takeaway
Generation is two-stage and rule-based: a **transfer grammar** (nine rule types) restructures the semantic representation into a target-specific underlying form (complex-concept insertion, feature adjustment, speech styles/honorifics, target TAM mapping, relative-clause strategies via a relativization-hierarchy dialog, collocation correction, genitive relationships, theta-grid adjustment, structural adjustment), then a **synthesizing grammar** (eight rule types: feature copying, spellout incl. table/morphophonemic/form-selection, clitics, movement, phrase structure, pronoun/switch-reference, word morphophonemics, find/replace) produces surface text. Work is front-loaded through a "Grammar Introduction" elicitation module; new rules per chapter then fall steeply. Reported effort: Kewa 21 sessions (grammar intro) + 30 sessions (Ruth) of ~1.5 h with a 32-year field linguist; Jula ~20 + ~20 sessions of ~2 h; Korean "two to three hours per verse" at first, "an entire chapter in less than half an hour" by the end. The transfer grammar is the hard part ("requires considerable experience dealing with ordering rules, feeding, bleeding").

### Cited Findings
- "The transfer grammar consists of nine different types of rules": Complex Concept Insertion, Feature Adjustment, Styles of Direct Speech, Target Tense/Aspect/Mood, Relative Clause Strategies, Collocation Correction, Genitival Noun-Noun Relationships, Theta Grid Adjustment, Structural Adjustment. Synthesizing grammar: Feature Copying, Spellout, Clitic, Constituent Movement, Phrase Structure, Pronoun, Word Morphophonemic, Find/Replace. — [PCJ 2014 §1.4–1.5](https://alltheword.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Philippine-Computing-Journal-2014.pdf); chapter 4 of the dissertation has the same list (§4.3.1–4.3.9, §4.4.1–4.4.8). — [Dissertation TOC](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf)
- Why transfer exists: "it's impossible to build a language neutral underlying representation that accommodates every language ... the noun phrases in the semantic representations are marked with semantic roles rather than grammatical relations. Therefore a rule in the transfer grammar must generate grammatical relations from the semantic roles ... many of the world's languages are clause chaining rather than coranking, so a rule in the transfer grammar must build appropriate clause chains from the coranking propositions". Theta-grid adjustment: "Every verb in every language has an associated theta grid ... The theta grids for the events in the semantic representations are very similar to the theta grids for the equivalent English verbs"; the Korean rule for walk inserts postpositions into source and destination NPs. — [PCJ 2014 §1.4](https://alltheword.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Philippine-Computing-Journal-2014.pdf)
- Spellout rules: "(i) simple spellout rules which add prefixes, suffixes, infixes, circumfixes, or a new word ...; (ii) form selection rules ...; (iii) morphophonemic rules ...; (iv) table spellout rules which group a common set of affixes together into a single rule" (example: Kewa tense suffix table). — [PCJ 2014 §1.5](https://alltheword.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Philippine-Computing-Journal-2014.pdf)
- Lexicon: "a linguist defines the features that are pertinent to each syntactic category ... each noun can be assigned a gender value, an honorific value, a class value, etc. ... the required forms are defined in the target lexicon ... Then lexical spellout rules are used to generate the various forms of each target word. All instances of suppletion are entered into the target lexicon manually." — [INLG 2012 §4](https://aclanthology.org/W12-1510.pdf)
- Collocation correction: "collocation correction rules ... permit linguists to map the concept carry to many different target equivalents based on the other concepts in the environment" (Tzeltal example from Larson 1984:89). Honorifics: "none of the other NLGs ... attempt to deal with honorifics"; Korean honorifics rules in §5.4. Kewa clause chains and switch reference in §5.3; Jula relative clauses §5.2; English questions §5.5. — [Dissertation §1.3.2, ch. 5](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf)
- Grammar Introduction: "we always start by working through a series of simple sentences which we call the Grammar Introduction ... various tenses, moods, aspects, illocutionary forces ... relative clauses, object complements, and adverbial clauses ... after working through the Grammar Introduction, the number of new grammatical rules required for each subsequent chapter of text dramatically decreased" (graphs for Kewa and Korean). — [PCJ 2014 §1.6](https://alltheword.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Philippine-Computing-Journal-2014.pdf); "The great majority of rules that need to be written for subsequent texts are related to particular lexical items—not general linguistic phenomena." — [Beale, LD&C 6 (2012)](https://alltheword.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Beale-DEL-Using-LA-2012.pdf)
- Effort: Kewa — "Dr. Franklin and I began meeting weekly in February, 2001, for approximately an hour and a half each session. We needed twenty-one sessions to work through the Grammar Introduction, and another thirty sessions to work through the biblical book of Ruth." — [Kewa project summary](https://alltheword.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Summary-of-Kewa-Project-with-ATW.pdf). Jula — "approximately 20 times between mid June and mid September of 2000 ... Each meeting lasted approximately two hours ... approximately 20 more sessions to complete that book [Nahum]." — [Jula project summary](https://alltheword.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Summary-of-Jula-Project-with-ATW.pdf). Korean — "Initially each verse required two to three hours of work, but by the end of this experiment, we could easily work through an entire chapter in less than half an hour." — [Korean project summary](https://alltheword.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Summary-of-Korean-Project-with-ATW.pdf)
- Difficulty: "Developing the lexicons and the synthesizing grammars for each of the four test languages was very simple and required little time ... However, developing the transfer grammars is considerably more complex and time consuming ... requires considerable experience dealing with ordering rules, feeding, bleeding, defining and setting features, etc. ... It is still an open question as to whether or not TTA is a practical system that field linguists can readily use without help from computational linguists." — [Dissertation §7.2](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf)
- 2014 direction: "deep source analysis techniques combined with shallow target analysis has proven to be a very efficient model"; a new type of synthesizing rule was added to minimise target-language knowledge. — [PCJ 2014 abstract](https://alltheword.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Philippine-Computing-Journal-2014.pdf)
- 2005 knowledge-acquisition modes: a TBTA expert works with one team, or leads a workshop for several teams (third mode cut off in the extracted text). — [MT Summit 2005 §3.1](https://aclanthology.org/2005.mtsummit-papers.9.pdf)
- Wycliffe Canada's description (2021): templates "for each Bible book to capture the original meaning of the Hebrew and Greek texts. Once made, they can work with any analyzed language"; staff "analyze a target language, including sentence structure, parts of speech, and the lexicon of words in the Bible". — [Wycliffe Canada, Word Alive, 2021-12-07](https://www.wycliffe.ca/wordalivenow/wycliffe-canada-leads-software-development-to-speed-bible-translation)
- The 2024 Wycliffe Canada update (search snippet only) says the team was "finishing a recoding of the TBTA software, with new features due for field trials" and "tested the use of TBTA in collaboration with other AI systems". — [2024 Summer TBTA Project Update (PDF, not fetchable here)](https://wycliffe.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2024-SUMMER-TBTA-Project-Update.pdf)

### Inferences
- The per-language cost that TBTA front-loads (elicitation questionnaire → transfer rules → synthesizing rules) is the same cost the bsb-add-books design assigns to "(B) target profile + (C) realization rules learned from aligned corpora and agents' decisions"; TBTA's evidence that post-introduction rules are mostly lexical supports learning (C) from data plus a small hand-written core.
- TBTA never made the transfer grammar easy for non-computational linguists (open question in 2010; no later paper claims it was solved); this is the most likely reason the model has needed a central team of linguists (ATW/Wycliffe Canada) rather than scaling by self-service.

### Gaps
- No rule counts per language were found in the texts read (the graphs are images). The rule-format examples (Figures 5, 7) are images not extracted.
- The software's current implementation language/platform after the 2024 "recoding" is unknown (older downloads were a Windows program with Access `.mdb` databases).

## Q3. Which languages were tested and what were the measured outcomes?

### Takeaway
Formal evaluations (2000–2010) covered Jula (Nahum), Kewa (Ruth), Korean (Luke 1–10, Ruth, Esther, Daniel, Nahum + health stories) and English (all 105 chapters): edited-vs-translated productivity ratios of 2.6–4.1 (Jula), ~6.7 (Kewa, one translator), 4.6 (Korean, 18 translators), blind quality judgements statistically equal to human translations, comprehension/back-translation tests passing. ## Q6. Comparison with UMR/AMR and with Larson / Beekman–Callow semantic structure analysis

### Takeaway
TBTA is a direct computational descendant of the SIL meaning-based tradition (Beekman–Callow/Larson propositions, Longacre discourse), not of AMR/UMR: its unit is the English-like proposition with one event, its "roles" are a small macro-role set, participants are tracked by per-verse indices and discourse-status labels, and it records speaker-hearer sociolinguistics, genre, salience and implicit-information types that AMR/UMR do not. UMR shares the aspect/modality-as-meaning stance and document-level coreference/temporal/modal graphs, and uses lattices to let annotators choose granularity, whereas TBTA uses flat closed value lists with many "N/A" cells. TBTA has no verb-frame lexicon like PropBank (it has per-sense case frames and theta grids) and no graph form; the arXiv "TTA"-like paper found in searches (Connor 2018) is a different, independent proposal that cites TBTA.

### Cited Findings
- TBTA's debt to the SIL tradition: feature definitions cited to Longacre 1995/1996 and Prince 1981; collocation ranges and Tzeltal "carry" from Larson 1984; "The meaning-based theory of translation underlies the TTA system." — [Dissertation abstract, §1.3](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf)
- TBTA's handling of implicit information and figures: `Implicit` NP feature (explanation of name, optional agent of passive, implicit argument/phrase, explain metonymy, implicit but necessary) and clause-level `Implicit Information` (cultural, situational, historical, background, subactions, argument); metaphors converted to similes with the point of similarity supplied. — [tbta_db_export README](https://github.com/AllTheWord/tbta_db_export/blob/main/README.md), [MT Summit 2005](https://aclanthology.org/2005.mtsummit-papers.9.pdf)
- UMR (from search results, not read in full): "aspectual categories are organized into a lattice that supports both coarse- and fine-grained aspectual distinctions", the same for "discourse relations, modality, number, spatial relations, aspect, and temporality"; sentence-level aspect and modality plus document-level coreferential, temporal and modal relations; a rule-based English AutoAspect reaches 76.17% recall on UMR events; a 2025 paper generates text from UMR. — [LREC 2022 UMR tutorial outline](https://lrec2022.lrec-conf.org/media/filer_public/0f/c4/0fc4ec2a-23f1-47f4-a4f8-856bf138b1d8/lrec2022_umr_tutorial_outline.pdf), [arXiv 2502.11973](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.11973), [arXiv 2603.24797](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24797)
- Connor (2018/2019), "A Concept Specification and Abstraction-based Semantic Representation", is an independent tree-line representation (`approach > [{past}, {agent} > Anne, {theme} > (teacher > stern) > the]`) that discusses TTA/TBTA as related work ("The Translator's Assistant [7], [8] (TTA), begun in 2005 ...") and contrasts itself with AMR ("AMR drop[s] pronouns ... our approach ... natively maintains all of the underlying concepts in the surface structure"). It is not TBTA. — [arXiv 1807.02226](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02226)
- A 2025 third-party critique of TBTA's annotations (from reproduction experiments) lists: God in Gen 1:1 marked "Routine" (presupposition conflated with anaphora, ~50–100 cases); Restaging/Integration/Exiting never used; Greek imperatives (Matt 5:44 ἀγαπᾶτε) coded Mood "Indicative" with illocutionary force carrying the command; aspect "Unmarked" overused (no Aktionsart classification); "Quadrial" has no attested language; lexicalized duals (שָׁמַיִם, מַיִם) coded Singular without note; schema lacks switch-reference, elevation deixis, Austronesian voice/focus, realis/irrealis, Bantu noun classes, serial verbs, tone, evidentiality. — [tbta-ai-framework critique.md](https://github.com/authenticwalk/tbta-ai-framework/blob/main/research/tbta-source/critique.md)

### Inferences
- For the bsb-add-books spec, TBTA's clause record is closer to what is wanted than UMR's graph, but UMR's lattice idea (coarse value with optional refinement) would fix TBTA's "N/A"-heavy flat lists, and UMR's document-level temporal/modal dependency structure is a better model for inter-clause relations than TBTA's subclause-type + conjunction concept.
- TBTA's mood and illocutionary force split (mood = epistemic/deontic strength on the verb; force on the clause) is a usable design; the critique's "imperatives coded Indicative" is a consequence of that split, not an error in TBTA's own terms.
- The critique's missing-feature list (switch reference, noun class, tone, evidentiality, realis/irrealis) is exactly the Kuni/Zinza profile; in TBTA these would be target-side (transfer/synthesis), with the source record carrying only what is needed to decide them (e.g. same-subject continuity is derivable from participant indices; noun class from the ontology's object concept).

### Gaps
- UMR and AMR sources were only seen as search snippets; Beekman–Callow's own text was not consulted (the dissertation does not cite Beekman by name; it cites Larson and Longacre).

## Q7. What could a new project legally and practically reuse, and what are the lessons of TBTA's history?

### Takeaway
Freely reusable now: the published design — feature inventory and value sets, the clause/phrase/word hierarchy, the four complexity levels and insertion-rule idea, the controlled-language authoring conventions, the "Grammar Introduction" elicitation method, the two-grammar rule taxonomy, and the evaluation protocol (timed edit-vs-translate ratio, blind pairwise quality, comprehension/back-translation). Reusable only with a written grant from All the Word: the semantic representations themselves (~11.6k–17.5k verses) and the ontology/grammar databases (no licence; an oral "freely distributed" statement). Not available: source code. Lessons: (1) the authored source analysis, not the grammar, is the scarce asset (one qualified analyst in 2005; still only a third of the Bible after ~20 years); (2) the transfer grammar never became field-linguist-friendly; (3) the representation had to be revised after each new language (Jula → conjunctions/proximity/relative propositions; Korean → speaker/listener features; Banjar → closing quotation frames), i.e. the "universal" inventory is empirically accreted; (4) explicating complex objects (artifacts, animals) and the flat ontology remained unsolved; (5) results are self-evaluated, from small narratives, by the system's authors.

### Cited Findings
- 2005: "The analysis system was created and subsequently augmented in this manner by a single user with experience both in Bible translation and computational linguistics ... the corpus is very large, so we will be seeking to add one or two more analysts"; "a major requirement of this project before it can be deployed ... is that the resulting semantic representations must be thoroughly checked by trained specialists." — [MT Summit 2005](https://aclanthology.org/2005.mtsummit-papers.9.pdf)
- 2010: "The ontology is the most problematic issue in this project"; artifacts like arrow/bow/axe and animals like sheep cannot be succinctly explicated; proposed fixes (multiple senses of SHEEP or a "which is an animal like a X" proposition on first mention) were untested; the ontology should be "restructure[d] into a hierarchical network". Using NSM primitives and molecules "1) made the development of the semantic representations considerably more difficult, 2) improved the quality of the generated texts, and 3) reduced the demands placed on the grammar." — [Dissertation §3.3.1, §7.2.1](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf)
- 2010: "Generally features and structures aren't added to the semantic representational system for one particular target language. However, if a structure or feature will be helpful to a variety of target languages, then the semantic representations may be modified"; five areas needing research: ontology, feature system, transfer grammar, semantic maps/typology, content of the representations. — [Dissertation §7.2](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf)
- 2010: "A myriad of sociolinguistic factors were certainly involved during the evaluation process, but those factors were beyond the scope of this research"; drafts are "at approximately a sixth grade reading level" and "use the target language's simple, basic terms and structures". — [Dissertation §7.1](https://alltheword.org/DownloadFiles/Reports/Tod-Allmans-Dissertation-for-website.pdf)
- 2012: the Linguist's Assistant framing — meaning-based elicitation questionnaire, immediate rule testing against elicited translations, then form-based elicitation and natural texts — as a language-documentation by-product. — [Beale, LD&C 2012](https://alltheword.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Beale-DEL-Using-LA-2012.pdf); the dissertation and the 2006/2012 papers are posted by ATW for download. — [alltheword.org/documentation](https://alltheword.org/documentation/)
- 2016: TBTA framed as building "a targeted bilingual corpus of about 300 sentences for each target language" and combining fragments, contrasted with example-based MT. — [tbta42.wordpress.com](https://tbta42.wordpress.com/)
- 2023: data "may be freely distributed" (oral); 2025: third parties restructure, embed and fine-tune on it and recommend a CC0 release, which had not happened. — [ryderwishart/tbta-csv](https://github.com/ryderwishart/tbta-csv), [current-knowledge README](https://github.com/authenticwalk/tbta-ai-framework/blob/main/experiments/current-knowledge/README.md)
- Coverage after ~20 years: 11,649 verses in the 2023 export; ~17.5k verses reported for late 2025. — [tbta_db_export tree](https://github.com/AllTheWord/tbta_db_export/tree/main/json), [databases/README.md](https://github.com/authenticwalk/tbta-ai-framework/blob/main/databases/README.md)
- 2021/2024 organisational response: a funded, multi-organisation team (Wycliffe Canada/OneBook/PBTI, ~25 people in 2022) to finish templates for all 66 books and recode the software. — [Word Alive 2021](https://www.wycliffe.ca/wordalivenow/wycliffe-canada-leads-software-development-to-speed-bible-translation), [2022 Annual Report snippet](https://www.wycliffe.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2022-Annual-Report.pdf), [2024 update snippet](https://wycliffe.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2024-SUMMER-TBTA-Project-Update.pdf)

### Inferences
- What bsb-add-books can take without asking anyone: the schema (Q1), as a documented set of facts; the authoring conventions as a checklist for an agent that writes clause records from MACULA + speaker layer (pronoun resolution to indices, one event per clause, implicit-info typing, deictic Time, speaker/listener demographics from the speaker layer, genre/salience from discourse structure); and the evaluation protocol.
- The main practical difference from TBTA's path: TBTA hand-authors the source analysis from English (NIV) with the originals "consulted"; a MACULA-derived record is produced by script from the Greek/Hebrew and can cover all 31k verses at once, trading TBTA's exegetical curation for coverage — TBTA's own experience (quality rose when representations were simplified and conjunction/proximity/relative-proposition conventions were fixed) says the conventions matter more than the richness of the inventory.
- If TBTA's verse data were licensed (e.g. CC BY or CC0), its 11.6k–17.5k curated verses would be a direct gold standard for a script-derived clause record (participant tracking, implicit information, speaker/listener, Time values) and a training set for agents; the oral permission is not enough for a CC0 export folder.

### Gaps
- No statement from All the Word or Wycliffe Canada about licensing intent for the data or the recoded software was found; a written request is the only way to settle it.
- No post-2010 published evaluation; no critique from SIL's AI teams or Scripture Forge comparing TBTA drafts with NMT/LLM drafts was found.
